### 1 A conversation of Fr. Kilian with Fr. Appiani

On January 1st, Fr. Kilian in his own name and in that of the Fathers, wished Fr. Appiani good fortune for the coming year.[^p38-1] As soon as he began the conversation, he warned him, if he had any request to make or anything to confide, to speak up at once in case the soldiers should prevent him from saying or hearing more. Fr. Appiani said that he had nothing to confide or request, except what he had recently asked Fr. Parrenin to obtain for him, namely a return to his church.[^p38-2] The Father revealed to him with what success this very petition had been made on December 24th, and he replied that he was prepared for anything but could not convince himself that his detention would last a long time. Thinking over the possibilities he had decided that very little had been alleged against him throughout. Fr. Kilian said that the Fathers certainly greatly desired that, with the help of God above, he should be set free as soon as possible. This was something which the reverend gentleman could easily believe was the wish of the Fathers for many different reasons, both because his detention or captivity was also a disgrace to us and very unhelpful to attracting unbelievers to embrace the Holy Law. Yet he feared that the Emperor would change nothing before an answer came from Rome. Since it may be presumed that his imperial dignity was conferred on him by Heaven, he is worthy of being listened to, and even if what he has to say about the peace of the people subject to him is disregarded as is his certain knowledge of the terminology and rites of his country, he has presented this information clearly, leaving aside the judgment of the Holy See as to their [the Chinese Rites] meaning. Both were preparing to discuss matters further when Fr. Kilian was called away on the pretext of a messenger from the Palace bearing an order for him. In fact, he was warned by the guards that he should observe more strictly the ban issued against his conversing with the prisoner.

### 2 Another with Doctor Borghese

After dinner, Doctor Borghese arrived, and asked Fr. Kilian whether he might find a way through him of speaking with Fr. Appiani.[^p39-3] The Father persuaded him that he should not lay both himself and Fr. Appiani open to further inconveniences through making inappropriate requests. He insisted nonetheless that a visit should be prepared for him by the Father so that he could meet him twice a month, at least for the sake of confession and of the indulgences twice every month which he said had been granted to him by our Most Holy Lord the Pope when he was setting out on his journey. The Father praised his desire for a devout and religious occupation and he promised his assistance all the more readily because he knew that Dr. Borghese had not even at Easter time in the year 1707 found a confessor, unless he had confessed his sins in the month of February to Father Sabino Mariani[^p39-4] or to Rev. Fr. Buonaventura[^p39-5] in the month of July.

### 3 Fr. Appiani is confined in a stricter prison and all access to him is denied

On January 2nd, Frs. Parrenin and Jartoux together went to perform the duty of visiting Fr. Appiani, but they were not admitted by the soldiers, even though at that time that gentleman still used the place where many young people came to decorate various glass objects.[^p39-6] But soon, with the severity of winter, efforts were made to obtain for him separate accommodation, and when another empty building could not be found, a humble apartment was put aside for his use, in which the crucibles and other similar objects necessary <!-- PDF page 40 | book page 31 | manuscript page 822 --> for a glass maker’s furnace had been kept until this time. When this had been made ready for living in, it was surrounded by an adjoining courtyard with a wall about seven feet in height; a gate was put into the wall, and in case anyone should come through it to make conversation, entry was prohibited by a notice in Manchu. By this residence within the Saffron Wall,[^p40-7] it was from the first day provided and for the future strictly assured for the priest that provisions and all that is needed for human life would not be lacking. | So each day our servant brings dinner and supper such as we ourselves frugally enjoy in our house to the gate, and afterwards receives the empty plates from a soldier. We also send a small jar of Chinese wine, which in the beginning the guards refused to allow in since according to Chinese custom wherever people are kept in a prison of the Tribunal, it is forbidden for them to drink wine.

### 4 The letter of the Bishop of Ascalon to Fr. Kilian

January 3rd. Fr. Monteiro informed Fr. Kilian by letter that Frs. Porquet, de Britto, and Capacci of our Societyhad not yet obtained permission from the Viceroy to go to Beijing to receive the permit.[^p40-8] About the other missionaries I had not yet heard whether they wanted to set out for the Court or not.

On January 4th, the Most Illustrious Bishop of Ascalon sent the following from Guangzhou to Fr. Kilian:[^p40-9]

Dear Father,

Yesterday, I received two letters from you at the same time. To the first, dated November 25th, I make no reply, seeing that the situation is different from that which was reported to you and so I reply only to the second written on St. Francis Xavier’s day after you received my own letter.[^p40-10] Since I am still writing <!-- PDF page 41 | book page 32 --> with an injured hand, I will content myself under three headings only, which certainly means that I will be briefer than I would like. In the first place Your Paternity devotes many lines in excusing yourself from blame in seeking a permit on my behalf, or at least he desires that the blame should be attributed to him alone. Was not the fault then at least formal (material?).[^p41-11]

### 5 He indicates his goodwill towards the Fathers[^p41-12]

I beg Your Paternity to read my letter in which I express deep gratitude to Your Paternity and the other Fathers for the singular kindness shown to me in this business so that you should see whether any bitterness could remain in me towards Your Reverence. None whatsoever remains, but rather fraternal love and I am even more devoted to you than before. So it is with this confidence that I press my point. Didn’t I write to Your Paternity or to Father Antoine [Thomas] the comparison with the doctor who killed his very best friend who was sick with a medicine which he believed would bring a cure? I believed and still believe both Your Paternity and your companions are my dearest friends, but I complain that I have been unlucky and I am looking for a remedy whatever it might be. On this matter, I think there is no more to be said, for I blush to speak of something which is plainly acknowledged in my letters.

Your Paternity says that you did not promise on my behalf that I followed the practices of Fr. Ricci by those words, etc.[^p41-13] This is true as far as the force of the words but more than false in the circumstances of the request, the time, and the one acting on my behalf. Besides, for the purpose of influencing the mind of the Emperor what advantage could the mention of the past bring if <!-- PDF page 42 | book page 33 | manuscript page 823 --> there is not included a protest against the past, and a promise for the future, at least in relation to the more certain and unambiguous decision of the Holy See? It is understood in this way in Rome and everywhere else, and it cannot be judged otherwise however carefully and cunningly the obvious sense be twisted by you to some other. That the Bishop of Beijing did it proves nothing, since by that time, I would not only have said I had followed the decree, but that I was following it and will follow it in the future.[^p42-14]

### 6 He explains certain things he said

I am surprised that Your Paternity is seriously saying that I have not promised, at least tacitly, that I would go to receive the piao, since the wording openly indicated it: that he is not bound to come as long as he is convalescing; therefore, it says that after I have convalesced, I am obliged to go. Next in the same letter Your Paternity seriously warns me that at an opportune time I should go to accept it, which is beyond any doubt reason for the mandarins compelling me to go, and there is no way I could decline this. Besides what was sought was not conceded, as Your Reverence together with Fr. Bouvet well remark.[^p42-15] So I can complain not only about the request of Your Paternity but about | my ill-luck. Certainly in my letters to Fr. Antoine I wrote that I requested to be allowed to remain in Guangzhou like Frs. Muñoz and Monteiro, but without making any promises on my behalf, and that if this could not be done, that it would be better to seek nothing?[^p42-16] Since Your Paternity and others at the Court had not yet been informed of the way that the mandarins were acting towards us, you thought that nothing had been done.

### 7 He says that it is not lawful for missionaries to remain in China if they are not exercising the

Your Paternity asks why I disapprove of the fact that some missionaries have stayed in their churches under the condition of not preaching against the Chinese rites. But what I disapprove of is this, that they should remain (if some do remain under this condition) as if they <!-- PDF page 43 | book page 34 --> were pure xiu dao, for they are thus understood by the emperor as pure xiu dao, that is, those who are in their houses not exercising the duties of the mission.[^p43-17] It was thus that the Prince[^p43-18] explained the condition to the Dominicans and Frossoloni,[^p43-19] from whom I heard this. To remain therefore in China under that condition, even for a time, is not lawful, as was declared by the consultors and the officials of the Roman Inquisition in Fr. Navarrete’s presence, and it is very mistaken to assert this, since it is not permitted to promise to do what one is not permitted to carry out.[^p43-20] If however they remain exercising other duties of the mission, but under condition of not preaching against the Chinese Rites, it does not pertain to me to judge whether it is in accord with or is against the edict, since interpretation of the edict is forbidden to me. But I am able to say that if the Rites are prohibited only by the Lord Patriarch, he himself could perhaps give a dispensation on his own authority for a time.[^p43-21] I can also say another thing, namely that the Lord Patriarch has these days called to account Fr. Buonaventura for violating his decree precisely in this way, since in a letter to the Lord Patriarch he confessed that he has said in his petition “I have followed the practices of Ricci,” and has written on other points to another (he <!-- PDF page 44 | book page 35 | manuscript page 824 --> does not explain who this other might be) saying that he accepted the piao on condition of not preaching against the forbidden rites. From these points, the Lord Patriarch concluded that the edict had been transgressed, that he had perjured himself, and that he was excommunicated. What does Your Paternity say to this?

### 8 He gives the reason why he has not set out for Rome

As for the other things which Your Paternity touches upon, I say that in matters of such great moment I do not act on what comes out of my own head alone. I told Frs. Monteiro and Raimundo my reasons (which should not be committed to writing), because of which it would not be fitting for me, now that my appeal has been rejected, to attempt anything against the edict, but rather to obey it with patience and make strong objections in Rome, a course of action which the two Fathers highly approved.[^p44-22] In addition, not only they but people in Macau, including the Lord Bishop, argued strongly that I should set out for Rome on the Lisbon ship, but I did not acquiesce in this. It was not because their reasons displeased me, but because this season four Manila ships have arrived, from which all of us Augustinians have not received a penny since all our subsidies were coming on a fifth ship which it is agreed left Manila at the end of October; we do not know where it has gone or whether perhaps it has perished. For this reason, without money I was unable to attempt so great a journey, since according to the sacred canons it would be a disgrace for a bishop to beg, and especially in Lisbon in a time of war.[^p44-23] Therefore let this be known to Your Paternity and Fr. Antoine. It is not possible then for me to go to Beijing to make a protestation about the practices (their edict on it is no joking matter), and by such a protest I can do nothing except provoke the anger of the King against me, and perhaps | against the mission too.

### 9 Why the Bishop of Ascalon wants to make for the Philippines

The only possible course left is to attempt flight to the Philippines, and by that route, if my strength permits, and indeed if God lets me go, to make for Rome. But it <!-- PDF page 45 | book page 36 --> is difficult for the mandarins to allow it since it has been promised by you in my name that I would go to Beijing as soon as I am recovered. For this reason it is fitting that Your Paternity or some other should inform the Emperor that I wish rather to conform myself to the edict than follow his declarations unless the Holy See agree, and for this reason I want to proceed to Rome in order to win the consent of the same Holy See where his declarations are firmly contradicted by the Bishop of Conon and others. I have written more than I had thought I could.

To sum up: it remains certain that I have had not the least doubt about the good will of Your Paternity and of the Fathers, but from doing too much, evils sometimes emerge. Also that I did not approve these words, “I have followed the practices of Ricci,” for they make a claim about the present and the future, and to assert the opposite is completely absurd. And that I will not go to Beijing, for, the edict remaining in full force, it would be brazen impudence.

Thus far the Most Illustrious Bishop of Ascalon. It is in conformity with the original, except that near the beginning, the Most Illustrious Lord wrote at least a formal fault, where I have put in brackets material, in order to correct a slip of the pen, and near the end, where the Bishop of Ascalon has, the edict (decretum) remaining in full force, I have corrected decretum to decreto.[^p45-24]

I so testify. Fr. Kilian Stumpf, sj, Apostolic Notary

### 10 An explanation of certain words of his letter

The reader should note that, when the Bishop of Ascalon speaks in what he wrote above about the Rev. Fr. Buonaventura and how it was not lawful to promise not to preach for a time against the Chinese Rites, he is speaking as passing on the interpretation of the Patriarch’s party, not as if he is endorsing something on his own account. For what Fr. Buonaventura wrote to the Lord Patriarch, and the matter itself, will be clarified in an appropriate place by citing the letters which Fr. Buonaventura himself wrote. As to the claim that the Lord Patriarch permitted people to promise for a short time not to preach according to the edict or decree or regulation of the Patriarch, the Lord Patriarch himself declared this to Rev. Fr. José Raimundo our Visitor in June 1707 and he is now in a position to personally testify to it in Rome.

### 11 A letter of Fr. Franchi concerning various matters communicated to him by the Bishop of Beijing

On January 8th, Fr. Franchi[^p46-25] from Wei xian[^p46-26] announced that what he wrote recently on 14 December 1707 he had written with the consent of the Illustrious Bishop of Beijing, and that he was adding the following to it. His letter goes as follows:

His Lordship the bishop told me that he was giving me leave to write down those things which he had lately communicated to me and I wrote part of them in my last letter to Your Paternity, but there a few extra points which I add here.

1. Fr. Antonio (Frossoloni) writes from Guangzhou that the Most Illustrious Lord Patriarch is in no way ready to make concessions in matters of his regulation, even as to remaining on condition of not preaching, and for this reason the Reverend Franciscan Fathers are not allowed to come to seek a permit.[^p46-27]

2. The Sacred Congregation of Propaganda sent to the Most Illustrious Bishop of Beijing some comments which had once been sent to Rome by the superior in Beijing against the aforesaid prelate, namely: i. that His Excellency wanted the Jesuit church in Beijing for his cathedral; ii. that he would not confirm the Christian women of Beijing; and iii. that he had removed himself from the Eastern Church to the Northern. After His Lordship read these things to me, he added that he on this very matter had offered sacrifices for many days on behalf of the Society.

3. That someone in the Philippines threatened His Excellency the Nuncio (the Lord Patriarch) | that Fr. Pereira would make sure that he would be flogged.[^p46-28]

### 12 The Bishop of Beijing compares the Lord Patriarch to Roboam

4. The Lord Patriarch wrote to the city of Linqing[^p46-29] that his affairs would have taken a different course if the Bishop of Beijing had been at his side and had the advice <!-- PDF page 47 | book page 38 --> of the Most Rev. Basilio da Glemona etc.[^p47-30] These things were said to me by the Most Illustrious Bishop, who, speaking of the business of the Lord Patriarch, gave the example of Roboam who neglected the advice of the elders and followed that of the younger people.[^p47-31] By a certain Franciscan father, the clerical gentlemen[^p47-32] were compared with the woman who, since she was not the mother, chose in Solomon’s presence that the child should be divided.[^p47-33]

Rev. Fr. Carlo (da Castorano, the Lord Bishop’s associate) wrote to the Lord Patriarch asking whether in these circumstances some explanation of the regulation could be given.[^p47-34] The Lord Patriarch made no answer. Rev. Fr. Carlo had spent the previous summer for the sake of his health as a guest in the Weixian district of our mission.[^p47-35] In the church of this town, there was a tablet in honour of Fr. Valat[^p47-36] with the letters shenwei.[^p47-37] He went and celebrated Mass in it. Yet in the month of November, he said to me that he had not in the aforesaid village made the regulation public, but only explained the (patriarchal) regulation privately to those families who were wanting to make their confession to the Fathers. I desire that this item should not be made known outside the College lest it come to the notice of the Father above mentioned, at least in the form of words which he used when speaking to me.

That this agrees with the original, I so witness.

Kilian Stumpf of the Society of Jesus, Notary Apostolic

### 13 Comments on the letter of Fr. Franchi

It should be noted:

1) That the reason why the Guangzhou missionaries did not go to Beijing to receive the permit given by the Illustrious Bishop of Ascalon and the Augustinian Fr. Hortiz in their letters was confirmed at the same time by Rev. Fr. Frossoloni.[^p48-38]

2) The clause in the second point about the sacrifices offered to God for our Society is obscure, and it seems to imply a complaint, as if the Illustrious Lord Bishop has sustained some insult at the hands of the Fr. Superior in Beijing. But there was no insult, even though we freely admit that on occasion such things were written about the said Lord to the Sacred Congregation, since no mortal person could persuade themselves that the Illustrious Lord was harmed in the eyes of the Sacred Congregation by raising matters which the Lord Bishop publicly claimed had been entrusted to him by the Congregation, even producing authentic documents which had been sent to him by the Sacred Congregation. For Rome had designated for use as his cathedral the Jesuit church in Beijing, and the Secretary of the Sacred Congregation (on instructions, as he said) had enlarged this to include even ownership.[^p48-39] But this matter is too well known for it to need a long answer, for the whole matter was entrusted to the Eminent Cardinal …, who on this matter consulted the opinion of the Most Serene King of Portugal.[^p48-40] The King (Dom Pedro) is acknowledged as the patron of that church and bishopric, and he said that he had never intended to transfer the ownership of the said church from the Fathers of the Society of Jesus; he had written in these terms to the Lord Bishop of Beijing, and an authenticated copy of this is kept in the Archives of the Society of Jesus in Beijing.

As to the matter of the suspension of the confirmation of women in Beijing, the Fathers of the Society of Jesus in a letter (of a certain day in a certain <!-- PDF page 49 | book page 40 | manuscript page 826 --> year)[^p49-41] petitioned the Bishop of Beijing, then living in the capital, lest danger should accrue to the whole flock because of the administration of that holy sacrament which the bishop said he had been urged to perform by the Sacred Congregation, even though there was no great necessity.[^p49-42] The Most Illustrious Lord could produce this petition which he had then received as evidence against the Fathers if there should be any need for him to do so, and there is another copy of it in our archives.

About removing from one house to another, why should the Fathers complain, who have received the same honour, and have practiced the same hospitality to the illustrious person of the Lord Bishop whether he wanted | to stay in their or another house of our Society? Perhaps one superior could be affected by jealousy, because that honour was afforded to another superior, with the result that he deserved well of the Most Illustrious Lord through the devoted services of his house. But how does this affect the Society as a whole? If a particular superior complained out of an imperfection of spirit, could not the remedy of a particular penance be applied without such complaints traveling to the other side of the immense seas?[^p49-43]

As for what was said in the Philippines, the Beijing Fathers should no more be held responsible than they have reason to, for example, be aggrieved with the Reverend Dominican Fathers in Fujian, because someone at the Minerva in Rome wrote many stupid things against the Beijing Fathers, or to be aggrieved with the bishop because someone who was a bishop published a book against the Jesuits.[^p49-44]

About the question put to the Lord Patriarch by Fr. Castorano in February and March, the same considerations arose, and with even greater force, as we shall see. For although Fr. Castorano is the associate and chaplain of the Bishop of Beijing, one must admit that the Lord Patriarch can hear proposals of this <!-- PDF page 50 | book page 41 --> type presented on his own initiative, which he could not do later when the public authority of others has intervened. Finally let the reader note that when Fr. Frossolini had long removed from the house of the Lord Bishop, Fr. Castorano, the bishop’s associate, promulgated the regulation not from the pulpit but from the confessional. On this point I assume that by now there can be no disagreement.

On January 10th, Fr. Kilian, in order to satisfy the desire of the Lord Bishop, who wished to know the true reason why the missionaries were prevented from going to Beijing, sent to Fr. Castorano, accompanied by his own letter, copies of the letters of the Bishop of Ascalon and Rev. Fr. Hortiz which were written in November and arrived in Beijing at the beginning of January. He could have been spared this labour if the letter of Fr. Franchi written on the 8th of this January, had come to Beijing within two days. Rev. Fr. Castorano replied to Fr. Kilian, as will be seen under the 27th.

### 14 The departure of Fr. Provana from China

On January 14th, Fr. Provana made his farewell to those in Beijing by letter, informing them that he had not been able to see the Lord Patriarch because the Captain General kept away by military guard those wanting access.[^p50-45] Fr. ### 15 Whether Fr. Müllener should return to the province of Huguang

Around this time, Rev. Fr. Müllener,[^p50-47] sent back by the mandarins, seems to have set out for the province of Huguang, but there is no evidence of this except indirectly from his letters, dated 9 and 10 February from Nanchang, to Fathers Parrenin and Castner, as will be noted under that date.[^p50-48] It is surprising that <!-- PDF page 51 | book page 42 | manuscript page 827 --> nobody wrote about his departure, since it was not done in secret and was decided after due consultation. In fact, it came about with the Lord Patriarch’s knowledge and to some extent by his disposition for him. For before Fr. Müllener left there, he consulted the Lord Patriarch by letter whether it would be appropriate to turn aside from his journey to go to the Court, and to see for himself if he would receive the residence permit, notwithstanding the protestation he would be obliged to make in public to observe the decree of the Patriarch, or if he would be ejected from China. With the approval of His Most Excellent Lordship, he finally went on his way. This indeed will seem to the reader beyond belief, but we leave the proof for it to Fr. Müllener himself. It suffices for us to say that this is exactly what Fr. Müllener himself stated before witnesses whose testimony is beyond question, as we shall see under the month of March following.

### 16 The letter of Fr. Castorano to Fr. Kilian

At the beginning of the Chinese year, I received your most excellent letter dated January 10th. I have informed our Most Illustrious Lord Bishop that Your Paternity wished His Reverence to know the real reason why the Fathers at Guangzhou | have not come to Beijing to ask for the permit, etc. For a long time, the said Lord had ceased from his doubt, on account of letters sent to him from Guangzhou. Further, he had written back to them about the intention of the Emperor in granting the diploma. And since the decree of His Excellency the Lord Patriarch has proved to be the obstacle, they would find him sympathetic if they should come to him. Although before he may have been ignorant of the reason for their hesitation, I can bear witness from the Illustrious Lord himself, that it had never entered his mind, nor had he doubted for a moment, as Your Paternity implied, that they were prevented from being able to come through the wiles of the Beijing Fathers. After the decree of His Excellency the Lord Patriarch, someone wrote to the Most Illustrious Lord that a Chinese book had been printed against the decree itself and in favour of the declaration of the Emperor. Because of this, the said Lord Bishop desired to know the author of the book, since it was claimed that your Fathers had been the authors or at least had been aware of it. I did not see the name which he said was mentioned in his letter. Perhaps it was omitted.

<!-- PDF page 52 | book page 43 -->

I was grateful to know so quickly the news about Fr. Antonino, so that I could offer the accustomed sacrifices on his behalf, for we were once companions on the road.[^p52-50] I cannot imagine why Fr. Buonaventura should ask for more than the usual suffrages, since not long back I heard news of his sickness; however, I heard no more. However, I think Your Very Reverend Paternity may be right in suspecting that he perhaps had doubts about the charity of certain persons. It is difficult when so many assertions are being made not to incline towards one faction of those who are speaking, especially when those who make the assertions are worthy of all trust. And I for my part give the utmost thanks to the Most High Lord God who disposed that I should come to Beijing, and brought it about that I should not lightly or quickly believe those things which are stated with every assertion of truth. I heard the business about Rev. Fr. Appiani from the Illustrious Lord, and that he himself is under guard in the glassworks. If there is an opportunity, convey to him my heart-felt compassion and that I remember him before God in my sacrifices, etc., feeble though they may be, so that he might be relieved of his burden.

That this agrees with the original, I so testify.

Kilian Stumpf, sj, Apostolic Notary

### 17 The Bishop of Beijing grants Fathers Castner and Liebstain faculties for administering the sacraments which he had formerly refused

On January 28th, the Most Illustrious Bishop, without being asked, of his own accord, gave faculties for administration [of the sacraments] to Fathers Castner and Liebstain, without demanding of them any previous oath to follow the regulation.[^p52-51] What his reason was for refusing them when they asked and granting them when they did not ask, the Most Illustrious Lord himself knows best. Here I offer to the reader his letter with its formula of concession from which it would appear that the Most Illustrious Lord feared the rigour of the Lord Patriarch, and equally dreaded to get in the way of the Gospel—for which eventually both he and we will be held to account. This is the letter to the Rev. Fr. Vice- Provincial, José Soares.[^p52-52]

### 18 His letter to Father Vice-Provincial

I was eagerly awaiting letters from Your Most Reverend Paternity before and during the Chinese New Year,[^p53-53] all the more because I had written to you twice, that is on 14 December with a bull of the reigning Pontiff Clement xi,[^p53-54] and on the 27th of the same month.[^p53-55] However, I was cheated of my desire; I do not know why, but I have my suspicions.

Your Very Reverend Paternity already knows how earnestly I try to be useful to you all, and all the more so concerning the task he laid upon me in the service of God and the ministry of souls. However, Your Paternity knows what I can do and I have a superior to whom I am accountable. So, Your Paternity ought to be sympathetic if at the first time of asking I replied in accordance with my obligation, which continues. But, so far as my own desire, my intention was, and is, that the said fathers should occupy themselves with the service of God and the salvation of souls. For that reason, I wrote in my letter of 27th December to Your Paternity that you should thank the Fathers; they are to write to me if there is anything I can do for them. Since in one respect I have fulfilled my obligation, | I anticipate his letters by following my own desire, and give faculties to the said Fathers to carry out their ministry in the same form as Your Paternity and the others do. I simply recommend that Your Paternity and the other fathers should keep this secret for as long as possible; and if they are obliged to tell someone, they should let me know before they reply. I would be delighted if these letters arrived at Guangzhou as soon as possible, and so I ask you to send them as soon as you can. Greetings to all the Fathers, etc.

The humble servant in the Lord of Your Most Reverend Paternity,

Fray Bernardino della Chiesa, Bishop of Beijing.

That this agrees with the original, I so testify.

Fr. Kilian Stumpf, sj, Apostolic Notary

### 19 The letter of the Bishop of Ascalon to Fr. Antoine Thomas

On January 28th, the Illustrious Lord Bishop of Ascalon[^p54-56] sent a quite long letter to Fr. Antoine Thomas[^p54-57] from Guangzhou in which first he complained that the Father did not reply to his letters, then that he had erroneously used the formula I have followed etc. in presenting the petition on his behalf. 3) He insisted that he was obeying the Lord Patriarch and would continue do so while in China. 4) He insisted that he would protest in the presence of the Emperor that he had not made the request with that condition I have followed &c. 5) He approved of the appeal of the 24 Fathers. 6) He would continue to support the right to appeal and its validity in general and in particular. 7) He asserted that if the Lord Patriarch should excommunicate those who do not observe his decree, they are to be considered as excommunicated, and recourse cannot be had here to royal chancelleries, etc. against similar violent actions. 8) He returned to his complaints saying that he believed the petition offered in his name to the Emperor has rendered his authority unsuitable for protecting the mission, and finally he concluded as follows:[^p54-58]

Frequently you Fathers and others have written to me, that my authority is useful and necessary for the protection of the mission. If this is so, and is not said just out of flattery, why do you act with all your strength so that my authority whatever it may be, should not only be reduced but totally taken away? Let Your Paternity tell me how I can escape from this threat hanging over me by going quiet. If things go thus with you who have been so committed to the Society and aroused the hatred of so many against you on its account, why do you not believe those things which have been done against the Lord Patriarch as he himself claims and complains ever since the aforesaid Lord showed himself openly hostile to you?

I have already proposed two courses of action to Your Paternity: 1) that in my name, in the Emperor’s presence, even at risk to me, they should say that I entirely obey the Lord Patriarch and not the declarations of the Emperor. 2) Since the first course seems dangerous for you too, it should be urgently petitioned that I should not be detained in China by the mandarins of Guangzhou, without admitting anything, and further without writing to me a single word of comfort. Your Paternity does not write. Rev. Fr. ### 20 Why the missionaries in Guangzhou are forbidden to go to Beijing by the Viceroy

### 21 Some remarks about the letter of the Bishop of Ascalon

The reader will notice, from this extract of the letter of the Lord Bishop, firstly the attacks made by the Patriarch’s people in Guangzhou against the Fathers in Beijing: how they wanted to be believed what the Lord Patriarch narrated there, by his authority and confirmed by oath, against the same Fathers, and how they admitted that the Lord Patriarch had shown himself openly as their enemy; this last point needs no proof. Those matters however, which are of the kind that are sworn to without any other proof, by this very fact remain suspect since they are sworn to by a person who on so many occasions has displayed himself as a most bitter enemy.

Secondly, I note that the statement of the Most Illustrious Bishop confirms that Fr. Kilian’s decision re: the letter of January 4th cited above was correct when he inserted the word “material” in brackets, since the Most Illustrious Lord corrected the error of his pen committed there, admitting that the petition made on his behalf, had been made through no fault of his, and through a “material” error.

Thirdly I note that Fr. Kilian certainly had written that he who was petitioning ought to respond to the Emperor personally. If the Most Illustrious Lord was not coming to seek the piao, or was going to leave China, the Father himself would take responsibility for any harm to the Lord Bishop if it should result. Likewise, the Lord Bishop writes truly and correctly here that he thought that the promise of the Father would be of no advantage to him if he was not allowed by the mandarins to leave, or was compelled to go to Beijing, which indeed no one at the Court up until now would believe would happen, since according to the edict of the Emperor, it remained permissible for any European to leave China. Why however the mandarins in Guangzhou prevented some from leaving, and placed others, though they had not asked to leave, under the necessity of leaving, the Lord Bishop said very well that he did not know, and the Fathers in Beijing would never know. The way that the mandarins behaved towards the missionaries was extremely various and inscrutable: while they prevented some from going, they sent away others who did not want to go. Among those <!-- PDF page 57 | book page 48 | manuscript page 830 --> sent away, some again were sent to Macau so that, by the express command of the Viceroy, held there were the Fathers the Abbate di San Giorgio,[^p57-67] Hervé,[^p57-68] and Frossoloni; this was done with an open and public display in the case of the Abbate di San Giorgio, who had expected that he would leave in a Portuguese ship and, had to be dragged back by force once on board, begging in vain for the protection of the royal standard.

### 22 What the Fathers thought should be done when they had received this letter

In regard to this, the reader should know that when this letter was read at Beijing the Rev. Vice-Provincial[^p57-69] collected the votes of all the Fathers as to what they should ask either out of friendship or by right to be granted to the Most Illustrious Lord Bishop of Ascalon. All voted unanimously that we should use every effort to obtain what he sought. But this letter dated 28 January reached Beijing around the beginning of April, in which month he had asserted that he would seek permission to leave and would depart, or, if permission were refused, would protest. Since no answer from those at Beijing could reach Guangzhou before the middle of May, by which time the ships for Manila would have departed, any remedy seemed to be useless | because it would be too late; it would be better to wait until it was known whether the Most Illustrious Lord either had left as he wrote he would do or had protested. Or, if he had not gone away or there has been no possibility of protest, to put off the matter for a longer consultation, since after the departure of the Manila ships in April or May until the following October or November, there could be a new face to the situation and there would be need for new plans. The result was that the Beijing people on the receipt of this letter wrote nothing to the Lord Bishop of Ascalon until July, since they always supposed that he had actually got away. They adhered to this supposition since the letters of all those in Guangzhou confirmed that the said Lord was making serious efforts to get away. Meanwhile, moreover, in the months of February and March, in which they had not yet received the above-mentioned letter, they had written answers on many occasions <!-- PDF page 58 | book page 49 --> to the earlier letters of the Lord Bishop, of previous months, which will be mentioned in their own place.

### 23 Some Fathers are summoned to the Court

January 29th. Fr. Monteiro, in a letter to Fr. Kilian[^p58-70] confirmed the firm resolve of the Lord Bishop of Ascalon to depart China and reported that the decree of the Emperor had arrived, by which Fathers Gonzaga[^p58-71] and Hinderer[^p58-72] and Brother Vieira[^p58-73] were called to the Court. Taking advantage of this opportunity there would also go to the court Fathers de Britto, Capacci, and perhaps Porquet of our Society. There was now a new opinion amongst the Religious other than the Jesuits that they wanted to go to receive the permit, in the belief that there was hope that it could be obtained without any condition.

### 24 Various rumours among those in Guangzhou

Finally in Guangzhou there had been written various discussion papers and submissions, principally by two missionaries, one a Dominican and the other an Augustinian, of whom the former defended requesting the permit as things now stood as being in effect to attest to having followed the practices in the past; this the Augustinian denied. The other argument was that the Emperor was in two minds: on the one hand, and this was to our advantage, he wanted to conserve the true religion in China; on the other, not to our advantage, he would not tolerate in China those who were unwilling not to act against Chinese rites, etc.[^p58-74] But it is better to refer to the text of the letter which is clear in itself:

The Bishop of Ascalon aims to go to Macau to embark in a small ship, of a type that they build there, which is getting ready to go to Manila. He fears that the mandarins will prevent him just as they have prevented others who asked <!-- PDF page 59 | book page 50 | manuscript page 831 --> to return to their countries. The reason is that in the piao they got from the mandarins it simply says they can stay here until they can go to request their piao from the Emperor; but it does not say that they can go to Europe.

### 25 The mandarins harass the missionaries

And here we cannot understand what those mandarins are doing: sending to Macau Fathers Lirot and Henry,[^p59-75] telling Fr. Müllener to return to Sichuan, and forbidding Luxán to leave China. From a copy of the letter of the mandarins, which before it was sealed was communicated to us, we learned that there was a zhi of the Emperor that the patriarchal party was to be closely guarded (a new affliction for the poor city of Macau) but there is no restriction on those who have a piao from the Emperor, and they can send to Your Reverences silver and letters; also, the two fathers and the brother are to be sent to Beijing. Until today this has been carried out. About those who have to go to receive the piao no decision has been issued by the mandarins.[^p59-76]

Without the permission of the mandarins nobody is allowed to travel without certain danger of being stopped during the journey, and the village heads are obliged to report any European who is newly arrived or is passing through …77 Your Reverence asks for testimonials and written evidence of the prohibitions of the mandarins on going | to request the piao &c. They could certainly be copied out by the xianggong of the churches but we cannot get hold of the originals without grave danger, and the mandarins will never give us authenticated copies of their documents. If someone should send for Fr. Porquet it would be dangerous and would incur considerable risks. Some of the other missionaries are running around placing their hopes in the belief that the Prince did not inquire about the Chinese Rites but gave the piao to anyone who asked for it. This presumption is based on the accounts of the Franciscan Fathers of Shandong, and even the letter of Miguel (Fernández) did not disabuse them.[^p59-78] Here they are saying that in spite of those Religious in the first audience declaring that in preaching the Gospel they would conform to the rules given by the Lord Patriarch, in the second audience the Prince gave them <!-- PDF page 60 | book page 51 --> the piao without asking them anymore about the rites and that Fr. Frossolini was not given the piao on any other grounds.[^p60-79]

If he were to divulge it, it would have been enough to get his Fathers arrested.

### 28 Some queries and requests put to the Lord Patriarch by the Franciscans

From the month of May 1707 to December of the same year, the Franciscan Fathers saw many things and heard more, and in the course of time came to know very well how much was being said without foundation by the Patriarch’s party, only to inject perplexity into minds since they were unable to produce solid truth. For this reason, or because they saw that this party was not seeking the things which belonged to Jesus Christ for the salvation of souls, they were keenly stimulated by a fierce spirit from within to preserve <!-- PDF page 62 | book page 53 --> the mission which had been watered by so much of their sweat. But fear got in the way through the headlong and violent condemnation directed at Fr. Buonaventura, together with other terrors which the patriarchal party from time to time threw at them. Finally they agreed in this, that the Rev. Fr. Commissary should put the following questions in the name of all to the Patriarch, and when they have received the answer they should come to a correct decision whether they should receive the permit with the approval of the Lord Patriarch and, by reason of the permit, through the Emperor, remain in China, or whether through the reluctance of the Patriarch they should neglect to get a permit and be obliged to leave the mission in ruins. The questions are as follows:[^p62-84]

### 29 First query

Queries put to His Lordship the Patriarch, to which we humbly seek answers:

1st Query. Is it possible for the missionaries to go to Beijing to ask the Emperor for permission to remain in China without any imputation of serious imprudence and temerity, since they have to protest and declare before all his ministers that they cannot give their consent to the declarations which the Emperor himself has made concerning the words Tian and Shangdi, the use of the tablets of the dead, and the cult of Confucius and the ancestors? We mention imprudence because it looks like claiming permission by means which one ought to deny. And temerity, because various decrees and oral communications have imposed the penalty of death on anyone who preached against his decrees. For the missionaries to appear before the Emperor and contradict him without having been summoned and compelled to do so is obviously going to make him angry, not merely against those who make the protest, but against Chinese Christianity as a whole.

### 30 Second Query

2nd Query: Assuming that it is possible to apply for the permit without imprudence or temerity, the question arises if the request and acceptance of the aforementioned licence without further examination would be contrary to the decree of Your Excellency of 25 January 1707. The reason for doubt is that, with the Emperor having declared that he did not want to permit anyone to reside in his kingdom who preached against his declarations and the Chinese rites, the aforesaid request and acceptance appears to be a tacit acceptance of not preaching against those declarations and rites. Moreover, the <!-- PDF page 63 | book page 54 | manuscript page 833 --> question also arises whether in the case that a missionary should happen not to have been seriously questioned he would be obliged to protest that he did not accept the licence conditionally on not preaching against the aforesaid declarations.

### 31 Third Query

3rd Query. Given that it is an absolute requirement for obtaining the licence to present to the Emperor a memorial in which the missionary professes that he has followed the practices of Matteo Ricci, is this tantamount to admitting to have preached in conformity with the aforementioned imperial declarations? The question arises whether this is contrary to | Your Excellency’s decree. The reason for doubt is this: because the Emperor does not care so much about the past as about the future. Hence the admission that one has followed Matteo Ricci is understood as a promise to follow him in future. This is how the Prince explained it on the evidence of Fr. Frossoloni and the Dominican fathers.

### 32 Fourth Query

4th Query. In so far as in the decree of Your Excellency referred to above there are criticisms of the tablets such as the Chinese use, the question arises whether the missionaries may permit them to the Christians, removing one character from the ones which the Chinese usually have or adding a character which they do not usually have. The reason for doubt is this: the tablets which might be permitted with those alterations could not be said to be such as the Chinese use; and furthermore, there is a question about which characters ought to be left out or added.

### 33 Fifth Query

5th Query. In so far as in Your Excellency’s decree referred to above, it is prohibited not merely to approve of and to allow the acts called Ji or sacrifice in respect of Confucius and the ancestors—the question also arises whether all the other acts which are not called Ji should be considered to be sacrifices; that is to say, genuflections, prostrations, and the offering of candles, perfumes, wine, and meats, which are also customary at burial places. Can these, or indeed should these, be allowed to Christians? A very specific declaration is requested concerning the 5th and 4th queries in order to free the missionaries from many grave scruples.

Before I report the answer of the Lord Patriarch given on 11 January to the above questions, I would like to observe that all these had been submitted already to the Lord Patriarch by 24 Fathers of the Society of Jesus in an appeal made in the month of May 1707, and in substance the core of the difficulties had been brought into the open by the answer given by Fr. Tomás Pereira around 8 March, to Fr. Monteiro, the Vice-Provincial, and Antonio da Silva,

<!-- PDF page 64 | book page 55 | manuscript page 834 -->

Vicar Apostolic of Nanjing, who with the desire and knowledge of, and by orders of, the Lord Patriarch had written to him.

### 34 The Father Commissary of the Franciscans passed on these queries and requests in the name of his men to the Lord Patriarch

Truly not one of those judged that such an energetic approach as that of the Reverend Commissary should be made to the Lord Patriarch. Let the reader if it pleases consider the problems raised by the Franciscans and their commissary and they will see how much thought and effort went into the queries or how much effort should have been put into the reply. For who would say that to make a petition boldly and imprudently together with a protest could be done without condemning the Imperial author by that protest? And who would absolve of rashness and imprudence those wanting more than twenty men to petition in this way and seek exile with the threat of death, and with the obvious danger of the destruction of the whole mission?

Nonetheless we do not accuse the Reverend Commissary as if he approached the Lord Patriarch more boldly than was right; rather we strongly praise him, because in his zeal for the procuring the salvation of souls and of calming his conscience and those of his men, he did not shrink from taking a gamble which until now had turned out favourably for no one who confronted His Excellency. But we owe him very many thanks because by submitting these questions for himself and in the name of his subjects, he openly showed how little the great majority of the Franciscans, in the matter of what had happened in Shandong, trusted in the word of Fr. Frossoloni and the interpretation of the Bishop of Beijing. For if it was true, or the provincial commissary believed that it was true, that his brothers had accepted the permit even though they had made a protest to the contrary, and the Prince had given the permit without the Prince or the Emperor paying any attention to whether they would preach against the Chinese Rites, why did he ask again if it is prudent and with minimum | risk for the permit to be sought by others under the same conditions? He was not unaware that the Lord Patriarch and his people wished everyone to think this was so and that his brother Frossoloni had twisted matters so strangely that he wanted it believed that the other brothers had received the permit, having made their protest; and that he [Commissary Bernardino Mercado de las Llagas ofm] had not received it, not because of the protest that he had made, but because of some other cause which was to be ascribed to the hidden judgments of God. This very thing was seized on by the Lord Patriarch as proof that the bad consequences that followed the publication of his regulation were not due to the regulation. It was useful for Fr. Frossolini that he should portray himself as innocent. It was advantageous for the eight Franciscans in

<!-- PDF page 65 | book page 56 -->

Shandong and Shaanxi that they should escape the blame which had swamped Fr. Buonaventura not long before—yet he was in a different way in the same boat as themselves. What the Rev. Fr. Commissary, spurning any advantage for himself and his people and the Patriarch, so bravely and even inappropriately insisted upon left no doubt that he himself and those with him were doubtful of the account given by those in Shandong, the testimony of Fr. Frossoloni, and the explanation of His Lordship the Bishop of Beijing.

Now to the answer of the Lord Patriarch. This was twofold. The first contained a promise that in the future His Excellency would suggest a solution to the queries proposed. The second was the following:

### 35 The response of the Lord Patriarch to the queries submitted by the Fr. Commissary

The Reply of the Lord Patriarch:[^p65-85] Reverend Father, the account given by Your Paternity in the first query is contradicted by what happened last June to the Fathers in the province of Shandong, and the Dominicans and Fr. Antonio da Frossoloni deny what you say or assume about them in your 3rd.
