February 1st. Again the Jesuits requested a residence permit for the Lord Bishop of Beijing, but without success. Then the Prince—with the intention of dealing with the matter of Father Sabino, at the personal command of the Emperor— gave orders to Father Grimaldi and to Father Parrenin to communicate to the said priest the following, as His Majesty’s own words:

“You have said that you are not of the household, nor one of the servants of the Lord Patriarch, and are not privy to his personal affairs, and that you had come to China independently of him, and that it was only by chance and fortuitously that you happened to be on the same boat with him. And now the Emperor says to you that the Lord Patriarch has never conducted himself in the manner becoming a distinguished ambassador, and has failed in all his undertakings and has acted in a way that is reprehensible. In addition, His Majesty says, that since on your own admission you are not in the papal service and that you were not chosen by the Supreme Pontiff to be part of the legation of Duoluo, that is of the Lord Patriarch, His Majesty has made this decision, which is the perfectly correct one, namely that the gifts which were recalled are to be retained until a real ambassador should arrive. But you, since you came in the company of the Lord Patriarch, are to go back likewise with him.”

Father Sabino asked whether this order came from the Emperor. The Jesuits replied that it was a formal order from His Majesty. And when he asked the same question again on February 2nd, Father Parrenin repeated these details to him word for word in his presence. And Father Superior J.F. Gerbillon, when he requested it, gave him in writing the imperial decree by virtue of which Father Sabino and Father Bouvet were recalled from Guangzhou to Beijing (see under September 1706).[^p309-1]

### 19. The date of departure and method of travel are specified for Father Sabino[^p309-2]

At the same time, the son of the Supreme Prefect of the province of Guangdong was summoned to the palace and was ordered by the Crown Prince to provide for Father Sabino one servant and a mule, which he was to ride to Guangzhou, setting out in three days’ time.

February 2nd. Father Sabino, when he realized that no further concessions had been made to him as regards the date or provisions for the journey, alleged <!-- PDF page 310 | book page 300 | manuscript page 512 --> that it was impossible for him to ride, even though in the previous year, when he was leaving Beijing on January 4th, and in the current year, when returning to Beijing on January 11th, he had ridden on horseback for several days. He submitted all sorts of paltry proofs of this impossibility. But they did not succeed in securing for him anything more than had been determined by the Emperor. He was given this advice, that he himself should provide some money to obtain the equipment which he knew he needed. Father Sabino did not acquiesce to this and asked to be told precisely by the son of the Supreme Prefect what was included in the Emperor’s orders. He found out, but to his own confusion. For the said man, losing his patience, sent to him the order from the Emperor which had been written with his own hand and authenticated with his own seal and the seal of the bearer.

Today, Doctor Borghese spoke to Father Parrenin on his own behalf, asking him to secure permission for him to leave Beijing. The Jesuit replied that he considered himself excused from such an undertaking, saying that he could neither make a petition nor give any advice in such a matter, which could end only in dissatisfaction on the part of Doctor Borghese and ill-will for the Society: he would make the request today and tomorrow would write that he had been driven out by the underhanded manoeuvres of the Jesuits. Rev. Fr. da Castorano had warned us today of this very thing, when he said that you simply cannot rely on what the good doctor says. He himself had suggested a plan to him, that he should write out with his own hand whatever he wished to have proposed through an interpreter in order to get permission to leave.

### 20. The Emperor withholds the gifts he usually gives to the Jesuits

Today, the Chinese year came to an end with a most solemn celebration, according to the custom of the country. But as for us, we were left out in the cold and devoid of all consolation. By now our faces showed signs of considerable stress from the prevailing state of confusion. By this time we had pleaded on our knees many times for a residence permit for our Lord Bishop of Beijing, but in vain; now we experienced a fresh feeling of shame at the undignified departure of Father Sabino, and amid all these signs of disfavour we were anticipating that the Emperor would send the usual gifts and presents according to the custom established over so many years. This would have been a consolation in our affliction. | But even these were denied us and not without certain proof of the Emperor’s displeasure.

February 3rd. This was the first day of the Chinese New Year. We went to invoke blessings on the empire and to pay our respects to the His Majesty with the customary ceremonies. We were admitted indeed by the Prince but with no signs of real welcome, to our great humiliation, and we can only say that we <!-- PDF page 311 | book page 301 --> were not ill-used. For the rest, we enacted the rites or genuflections in honour of His Majesty in the upper court, in the way in which as a mark of honour we had been permitted in former times. But it was under the hostile gaze of the Prince who was watching us.

In Nanjing affairs were more tranquil according to what Father da Silva wrote to us: “On the first day of the Chinese year all the Christians came to wish a happy New Year to His Excellency, who was seated in the Ting in the place of honour in his episcopal seat. He called the Christians one by one on their knees to receive a holy blessing. Then he gave each one a large Caravaca cross.[^p311-3] On that day he must have distributed more than two hundred crosses. On this occasion the Reverend Montigny[^p311-4] said to him that it was a good opportunity to bring out a second time for veneration the portrait of the Holy Pontiff but the Lord Patriarch did not agree.”[^p311-5]

February 4th. On account of the New Year, and of the refusals so often repeated, the Most Illustrious Bishop decided that the Jesuits ought not to renew their appeals today. Nevertheless the Fathers of the College, Grimaldi, Pereira, and Antoine Thomas, went alone and on bended knee asked the Prince to have pity on an excellent old man etc. When he heard this from another source, the Emperor said, speaking to the Prince who had remained silent: “Are the Europeans still making their petitions?” “They are,” replied the Prince, “and persistently.”

### 21. He orders permits to be prepared for the missionaries

“Very well,” said the Emperor, “I want this favour to be granted. And let a number of copies of the permit be prepared to be taken with us to the South. We will grant these according as we judge they should be given; if we refuse any individuals, let them not dare to petition on their behalf.” Who can say who was happier with this news, the Most Illustrious Bishop or the Jesuits? The Jesuits immediately hurried out of the Palace to the northern residence to bring the good news <!-- PDF page 312 | book page 302 | manuscript page 513 --> to His Illustriousness, who on the following day, February 5th, in company with all the Jesuits gave thanks to the Emperor in his villa for the favour which had finally been secured.

February 6th. Father Sabino departed, carried in a sedan chair. He should have left on the 4th but because he absolutely refused to ride and wanted to be carried in a chair, to this end he had sent to his conductor the sum of 50 patacas. But despite this, on account of the celebration of the Chinese New Year, chairs were not available. The conductor deferred the departure until the 6th, on which day he provided him with a sedan chair and returned the 50 patacas. He asked us repeatedly to make sure that through our servants it should not come to the knowledge of the palace staff that there had been extra time as well as other concessions extended rather generously in favour of Father Sabino.

When Father Sabino Mariani was about to leave the college, he tendered 10 patacas with the intention of paying for the food for himself and his servants over a period of 27 days. The Fathers refused to accept this. But he left them with Doctor Borghese, together with some trifles he had brought with him from his travels with the intention that they should be offered to us again at some suitable time. This was something which he was careful to do. He met up with Father Antoine Thomas in his room, and when he saw that he resolutely refused this money, he departed after leaving it in the anteroom. But the Father did not allow himself to be circumvented even in this way, and after the space of one or two days, he returned the entire sum to the doctor’s dwelling. He said emphatically that we would never accept it. Then he caused to be carried to the same gentleman the few miserable pieces of furniture which Father Sabino had left behind.

February 7th. Nothing worthy of note happened at Beijing, except that our Most Illustrious Bishop was getting himself ready to complete his return journey, quite ignorant, as we were, of what was being perpetrated by the Lord Patriarch at Nanjing. It is worthwhile transcribing it accurately from the account of Father da Silva.[^p314-8]

On the 7th of February during the morning the Lord Patriarch wrote me a letter in which he summoned myself and the Father Vice-Provincial to be at his house three hours later. I was already very much afraid that he had called us to publish some sort of decree against the Chinese Rites. We both got there at the appointed time and entered the Lord Patriarch’s room. We found assembled there the following gentlemen: François Montigny, French missionary from the church of Nianzhou; Pierre Hervé, French missionary from the church of Chizhou;[^p314-9] the Abbate Ilarione Sala and Francesco, Abbate di San Giorgio, both companions of the Lord Patriarch; Bartolomeo de Carvalho from Manila;[^p314-10] and the chancellor Andrea Candela; making us eight in all. Fr. Luxán[^p314-11] did not join us because neither he nor the Lord Patriarch requested that he should assist with this act. After all were assembled the Lord Patriarch, seated like a king on his throne,[^p314-12] | gave a brief proclamation in which he said that he had called us together in order to put us on the right path regarding the Chinese Rites, as he had wished to do for a long time. When this proclamation had concluded the Chancellor distributed to each of those present in turn a sheet of paper fastened with the seal and insignia of His Lordship, on which was written the decree that had to be published. Then, getting to his feet, by order of the Lord Patriarch he read it in a loud and clear voice. The form of the decree is as follows:

<!-- PDF page 315 | book page 305 -->

### 24. A decree from the Lord Patriarch as to the manner in which missionaries, when questioned about the Chinese Rites, are bound to reply

Charles Thomas Maillard de Tournon, by the grace of God and of the Apostolic See, Patriarch of Antioch, Domestic Prelate of His Holiness D.N. D. Clement XI, by Divine Providence Pope, Assistant at His Pontifical Throne, also Consultor of the Venerable and Holy Roman Inquisition against the evil of heresy, and in the East Indies and Chinese Empire and neighbouring kingdoms and islands Commissioner and General Apostolic Visitor with the powers of a legate a latere, etc.

To the Most Reverend Lords and Brother Bishops, to Local Ordinaries, to Vicars Apostolic, Rev. Pro-Vicars, and also to Missionaries Apostolic in these kingdoms of the Chinese.

Blessings forever in the Lord!

Since we have heard that workers for the Gospel are being summoned to answer questions on matters touching our holy religion, so that they may be enabled to separate the good seed of the Word of God from poisonous growths of superstition in their public profession of faith, and so that we may be witnesses and speakers of the truth while speaking with one voice, and avoid scandalizing those who are straying in the wilderness, and with one voice and one heart glorify God, that God who is not the God of dissension; remaining in union with the mind of the Holy Apostolic See and holding firm to the decision (about which we are quite certain) which has emanated recently from that same Supreme See and from His Holiness Clement XI by Divine Providence Pope, after studies devoted to it over so many years of toil spent in the examination of the controversies which have for so long troubled this mission: we have determined, in accordance with the duty of our office, to propose, declare, and lay down a certain rule and law to be observed in the future by all concerning the questions which, they say, are bound to be asked. With that greater power which we enjoy, even that of a legate a latere, we propose, lay down, and decree and require that it is to be observed in all respects by all the missionaries in the mission, both seculars and regulars of no matter what Order, even the Society of Jesus.[^p315-13]

If they should be questioned whether they consent to Chinese doctrine, laws, rites or customs in general, and promise not to oppose this or that, nor to write or preach against them, then they will be bound to reply that insofar as <!-- PDF page 316 | book page 306 | manuscript page 515 --> these agree with the Christian religion and can licitly and properly be practiced together with it, they will be bound; otherwise, they will not.

If they should be further questioned as to what things there are in the Law of God which are not in conformity with Chinese teachings, they will reply that there are several such things. And if they are required to give examples, they may mention in reply anything which occurs to them: the taking of omens or sacrifices or Ji which are made to Heaven, Earth, the sun, the planets, Spirits, the inventors of the arts, and others.[^p316-14] For it is lawful for Christians to offer sacrifice to God alone who is the source of everything, and from whom they receive every good gift, and misfortunes also.

If the questioning comes down to the Ji or sacrifices of Confucius and the ancestors,[^p316-15] they will reply negatively. We are not permitted to offer those, nor tolerate them among those who follow the law of God. Likewise, negatively as regards the use of the tablets of the dead ancestors | customary among the Chinese. Also negatively if they are questioned whether Shangdi and Tian are the True God of the Christians. If they are questioned why they have this opinion about the matters mentioned, they will reply that it is because these practices are not in conformity with the worship of the true God, and it has been so determined by the Supreme See which is the infallible rule of Christians in matters of faith. If there is a question as to the time of the decision, everybody should know[^p316-16] that it was issued on 20 November 1704. If finally someone were to say, how can we be quite sure of this? The answer is that it is established by the declaration regarding this made by the Patriarch of Antioch, our Superior, who is in himself constituted a mouthpiece[^p316-17] of the Supreme Pontiff in virtue of his faculties, and we are bound to believe him.[^p316-18]

### 25. He imposes penalties on anyone acting otherwise

And thus, in virtue of the apostolic authority committed to us, unworthy as we are, and also with the powers of a legate a latere, we decree, enjoin, and order this to be obeyed by each and every one of the bishops, vicars-apostolic, local ordinaries, missionaries, and priests, secular as well as regular, including those of the Society of Jesus, who are living now in this China mission and will live here in the future, under the penalty of excommunication latae sententiae[^p317-19] so reserved to the Apostolic See and to ourselves, that not even under pretext of any privilege whatsoever communicated by Popes, even to the Fathers of the Society of Jesus, is it possible for anyone to be ever absolved except at the final moment of death.

Solely for the purpose of achieving this effect, and not in other cases, in so far as it is necessary, we suspend and revoke every privilege whatsoever, in virtue of a special faculty which we enjoy for this purpose.[^p317-20] Also withdrawn is any faculty to avoid or otherwise interpret this our command under pretext of the decree of Alexander VII, Pope of holy memory, which was issued in 1660,[^p317-21] and even under pretext of some great peril. For by a special indult,[^p317-22] the interpretation and the exposition of the Apostolic Constitutions has been committed to us.[^p317-23] And on account of this we declare that notwithstanding the said decree, or any grave danger whatsoever, this is to be observed, taught, and given in answer by everyone wishing to remain in this mission or to enter into it, under the aforementioned penalty to be incurred ipso facto, since the greatest good of religion and of the mission consists in the glory and the honour of the Divine Spouse, which Christ acquired by His Own Blood and wove into a seamless garment.

Given at Nanjing, 25 January 1707, in the seventh year of the Pontificate of Our Most Holy Lord Clement XI by Divine Providence, Pope. [Seal affixed here]

Charles Thomas, Patriarch of Antioch, Apostolic Visitor.

Andrea Candela, Apostolic Chancellor of the Sacred Visitation, and Missionary Apostolic. Act. Vis. Ap. Fol. 8324

When this document had been read out by his chancellor, the Lord Patriarch said that any one who had anything to say should do so, and then they should swear to observe it and sign to that effect. I who was the first to speak predicted the collapse of the mission and danger to all the missionaries, and sought permission for a day’s grace to read the documents at home, and then I would sign. The second to follow was the Vice-Provincial Father José Monteiro, who made a public request for the Lord Patriarch to show us all the decree of the Holy Pontiff. He replied that it was not necessary to show it (in a letter dated … he said “… We are not obliged to.”[^p318-25]) Then the Father Vice-Provincial, after making a speech in which he showed that the mission would be ruined, swore and signed. The rest (with the exception of the Chancellor) swore and signed the document which the Lord Patriarch held in his hands. I went home and read the document at my leisure, and seeing that I was the odd man out, the only one who had not signed, it did not seem right to me to take to myself all the responsibility in so serious a matter, and so the following day I took the oath and signed.

This is what the Most Rev. Fr. da Silva wrote in Nanjing. But let us return to Beijing. |

February 8th. That extraordinarily savage letter was delivered to Beijing by the ordinary courier, the one which I transcribed in its entirety under January 18th. When it first came into the hands of the Father Superior, he said to the other priests that since it was now late in the evening, they should assemble on the following day to give their opinion as to a suitable response to be given in these circumstances.

### 26. After they had received the Lord Patriarch’s letter, the Jesuits consult the Bishop of Beijing

On February 9th, everyone agreed that the said letter should be communicated to our Most Illustrious Ordinary, who was still in our house although ready to leave that very morning for his house in Linqing. Therefore Fr. Superior J.F. Gerbillon, Fr. Antoine Thomas the Vice- Rector, Fr. José Soares, Fr. Joachim Bouvet, Fr. Dominique Parrenin, and Fr. Jean Régis[^p319-26] went to His Most Illustrious Lordship, whose associate Rev. Father Carlo da Castorano, Apostolic Notary, was present. After showing [Castorano] the said letter of the Lord Patriarch in the original, they made a public oral declaration that they were entirely innocent of each and every of those crimes of which they were being charged by His Excellency in this extremely venomous calumny. The Most Illustrious Lord read a part of the letter, and then wanted to hear it read in its entirety by his associate, the Apostolic Notary, in the presence of the Jesuits. He also asked for and was given an authentic transcript, which he took away with him. The Illustrious Bishop kept on saying several times: “I believe that you are innocent, but that is not enough. Your innocence must be clearly demonstrated to everyone, even though you are not in need of vindication in the eyes of God.” At about 10 o’clock in the morning, the Most Illustrious Lord set out on his journey.

February 10th. There was a consultation regarding the response to be made to the Lord Patriarch, to his very bitter and terrible accusations. The majority opinion was that just now we should give way before his rage, and that we should respond briefly and calmly, and not lodge any complaints with the Lord Patriarch whom we could see was under the influence of extreme passion, and so we could not hope that he would either accept any defence, or acknowledge our innocence, or give us satisfaction. The Father Superior, Jean François Gerbillon, wrote back a brief reply saying that we did not deserve any such charges, and that we would submit our just defence to the forum of a competent judge.[^p319-27]

### 27. Their response

The Reply sent by Father Antoine Thomas.

Most Excellent Lord,

I was dumbfounded when I read Your Excellency’s letter dated Nanjing 18 January to the Fathers of the Society of Jesus living in Beijing. In it I read that there were imputed to us without any foundation such tremendous and atrocious crimes against the Christian religion and the Holy See as could scarcely be believed true even of men of the most desperate way of life who had abandoned <!-- PDF page 321 | book page 311 | manuscript page 517 --> all conscience and religion. But I am not suffering from any feelings of distress, for the testimony of a good conscience and also innocence sets me apart from such crimes. On the other hand I could not but rejoice because we were receiving a sign that our labours endured here on behalf of religion for so many years were not | displeasing to God and which He wished to be rewarded with the same recompense in this life as he gave to Christ, His Only-Begotten Son. For we are no better than Christ Our Leader and our happiness is to follow him in the midst of reproaches as he goes before us, trusting in the help of Him who, to bring the truth to light, will be with the Holy See when this cause arrives at its Sacred Tribunal.

### 28. With modesty, they profess their innocence

Meanwhile I am asking Your Excellency and in the name of my subjects that he will deign to accept our most humble entreaties by which we beg him to be willing to be acquainted with the genuine truth about these matters. We are able to provide proof of this in the Supreme Tribunal and in the whole world lest persecuted innocence should remain condemned in the whole world and before posterity, especially if here on earth the real truth, emerging from the darkness, shines out for the Vicar of Christ and for Your Excellency. On account of illness I write only briefly with my hand trembling from my infirmity. Otherwise I shall write at greater length when I am well. I commend myself to your holy sacrifices and most humbly request your blessing.

Beijing 4 March. 1707.

The most humble servant in Christ of Your Excellency,

Antoine Thomas[^p321-29]

It should be noted here: 1. that this Father’s reply was considerably delayed, because although he began to give consideration after February 10th as to what was the best reply to give, he became ill which from day to day stopped him from writing; 2. that the Lord Patriarch’s letter which is transcribed just above this, had this inscription on the outside face: “To Rev. Fr. Antoine Thomas, Missionary Apostolic of the Society of Jesus,”[^p321-30] while on the inside at the end it was inscribed “To Father Antoine Thomas, SJ, Beijing.” Therefore His Excellency did not want to acknowledge in writing that this Father was the Vice-Rector or the Substitute for Father Vice-Provincial, which was something of which he was <!-- PDF page 322 | book page 312 --> officially aware. If this happened merely by accident, we do not know. Nevertheless it is highly unlikely to have happened by accident because he had addressed the others to “Father J.F. Gerbillon, Superior of the Jesuit Fathers, SJ and to the other Fathers,”[^p322-31] thus gratuitously increasing the authority of one and without reason diminishing the authority of the other.[^p322-32]

On this same day, February 10th, the Lord Patriarch wrote another letter to the said Father Antoine regarding the decree published in Nanjing, and in this one too he wrote a similar inscription: “To Rev. Fr. Antoine Thomas, Apostolic Missionary, of the Society of Jesus.” This will be recorded together with the Father’s response on March 9th.

February 11th. With the publication of the decree, other matters of business were also being pressed by the Lord Patriarch, but the couriers, according to the Chinese custom, were taking holidays; meanwhile on 10, 11, 15, and 17 February, various letters had been prepared by Father da Silva and Father Vice-Provincial to comply with the orders of His Excellency by sending them as soon as the seals of the Tribunal were reopened.[^p322-33] I will discuss each of these under the month and day when the response from Beijing is recorded.

### 29. They refuse the request of the Crown Prince for the sacred vestments to be used in a comedy

February 13th. The seasonal comedies were all the rage at Beijing and on several occasions the Prince sent the man called Pursama, who has been frequently mentioned round our three Churches, to bring him our sacerdotal vestments which we wear when going to celebrate at the altar on the more solemn feasts. We said that we were not able to convert them to secular uses because they are considered so sacred by us on account of their use in worship of the Most High God, that it cannot be permitted for them to be worn even by those lay Brothers of ours who are known to the Prince. To make sure that our answer was not misrepresented, the Father Superior himself, Father J.F. Gerbillon, hurried out to the Prince at the villa to answer him there personally, or to endure the reproaches if he were angry. But the Prince took the refusal fairly mildly saying: “We regret that we are not able to have your vestments, because they would have been most suitable for our comedies etc.”

February 19th. The Emperor was about to set out on his travels, and because the Emperor was preparing to travel to the South, Doctor Borghese began to press for his discharge, or if it should happen that he was not to be released that at least he might be permitted to travel through China in the Emperor’s entourage. Because he himself regarded this as desirable and no one was able to begrudge it to him, on this very day he finally overcame his hesitancies and for the said purpose he presented to Father Antoine a request for release written in his own hand in the form shown below:

I have now been idle for four months, and if the Emperor does not wish to avail himself of my services in the future, I am applying for permission to proceed to other regions as soon as the rivers are flowing.

19 February 1707.

Giovanni Borghese

20th and 21st February. The above-mentioned Fathers who had just arrived travelled to the Villa to present themselves to obtain the permit, and among others Fr. Antoine Thomas went with them to see how much could be requested on behalf of the doctor.

February 23rd. Since Doctor Borghese had pleaded even to tears with Father Antoine on the evening of the 21st to secure permission for him to depart, the Jesuit finally agreed to try again, even though recently he had found the eunuch less accommodating; he went, and all of us with him to bid farewell to the Emperor who was on the point of leaving. The petition from the doctor was presented to the eunuch Zhang Qilin. He listened, returned, and gave no answer. When he was asked what consolation there was for the doctor, as though he was giving a response on his own behalf, he poured out a torrent of abuse against the petitioner and reproached the Jesuits for involving themselves in a request of this kind. 2. He said that the fellow seemed to be irresponsible, for of his own choice he decided to remain in Beijing, and now after a few months he is pressing for the right to leave, as if he had been kept there by force. 3. The reason he is getting ready to leave is childish, namely that his skills are not being put to use. Does he not know that there are here many Europeans who sometimes wait for a whole year or more for orders from the Emperor?

While all this was happening within the orchard of the Villa Doctor Borghese, who stood waiting outside the gate, was upset because he had not been brought inside and even more so when he found that we were bringing an answer in the negative. However it is not our right to introduce anyone else there, for in these matters it is necessary to observe the customs of the place which the mandarin with the soldiers and the gatekeepers ensure are observed strictly.

### 31. Doctor Borghese is extremely upset by the denial of permission to leave

Further, the doctor from this day onwards began to be driven almost into a state of madness by his desire to leave, and tried many things which were unbecoming and which did not cause him shame because he was unconscious of it, but which certainly caused us very great shame in the eyes of the Chinese and Manchu servants and people outside the house.

[February] 24th. The Emperor began his journey towards the province of Nanjing, taking with him from among the Jesuits Fathers Joachim Bouvet, Dominique Parrenin, Pierre Jartoux, and Brother Domenico Paramino. It was a cause of great satisfaction to us, that in spite of the great humiliation brought on the Europeans in the preceding months, His Majesty deigned to take some of them with him, and to make use of their skills. It was said that they were being taken to determine the levels for a new canal which was to be opened up at great cost. |

February 25th. Fr. Antoine Thomas was making preparations to try to cross over to the province of Liaodong, still unaware of the arrangements that the Most Excellent Lord Patriarch had made concerning that province with the Lord Bishop of Beijing and with the Most Rev. Miguel Fernández, the Franciscan missionary in Linqing. It seemed to Fr. Antoine that the most important reason for going there was that those three Christians who had been sent there into exile would either disturb the native Christians on account of the penalty they had incurred, or that the pair, Thomas Gu and Peter Wang, would pervert them by their usual deceits and evil example. But the Father was prevented from leaving by a sudden violent urinary infection, and wanted Fr. Antonio Provana to substitute for him on this journey. But the latter contracted a fever on February 28th, which if not malignant nevertheless was very severe and not intermittent.
