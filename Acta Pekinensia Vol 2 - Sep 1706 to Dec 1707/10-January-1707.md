In Beijing nothing augured well for a good New Year’s Day. Everything was plunged into deep mourning, both because of the recent horrible sentence of the Emperor and because of a lively apprehension of the enormous evils, which from month to month, or rather from day to day, seemed very likely to follow in the year to come.

### 1. The Lord Patriarch in Nanjing brought out an image of the Supreme Pontiff for public prayer and what the Christians understood by it

In Nanjing minds were more at rest and more joyful matters occupied them, or at least they did not contemplate that their lives were about to be turned upside down, nor that it would be through what was about to happen to them. This is what happened there.

January 1st. The Most Reverend Father Antonio da Silva, Vicar Apostolic of the place and of the province, wrote in these words:[^p286-1] “On the first day of January 1707, many Christians came to hear the mass said by the Lord Patriarch[^p286-2] at the Dong Tang.[^p286-3] And when mass was finished a panel or portrait of the reigning Holy Pontiff, Clement XI was put on display in the great ting[^p286-4] or guest hall. There, in public he went down on his knees and adored the portrait of the Holy Pontiff. Then there followed all the reverend clerics who were there; finally all the Christians one by one performed their adoration of the portrait of the Holy Pontiff.”

The more knowledgeable of the Christians were pleased at this adoration (Father da Silva used these words in the broad sense) of the picture of the Supreme Pontiff, reasoning that since this was not wrong when accorded to a mere man who was absent, living or perhaps dead, their prostrations before the image of their Master Confucius were not wrong in themselves; therefore they were confident that the Lord Patriarch would never forbid that what he had done could be done before a Chinese image, since he had not only permitted it but taught it by example before a European image. But they were seriously deceived, for in this very month in the same house, even more than <!-- PDF page 287 | book page 277 | manuscript page 500 --> usual, with greater fervour, he carried on the task of forbidding the Chinese rites. This became public after January 20th, and was the beginning of so many and such great troubles for the missionaries.

### 2. Father Bouvet with Father Sabino comes to Beijing

January 11th. Fr. Sabino together with Fr. Bouvet arrived at Beijing from Guangzhou, and immediately went to our church within the Saffron Wall.[^p287-5] Their arrival had already been announced to the palace by Pursama, the escort of both men. In the same church was a certain palace attendant who had been sent to await their arrival. He informed Father Sabino, who was in the act of getting down from his horse to pay his respects, that he should get on his horse again and go immediately to the College, where by order of the Emperor he would be a guest. While Fr. Bouvet and Father Sabino remained each in their own house, Pursama went alone to the Emperor, who was occupied in hunting not far from the Great Wall, to inform His Majesty about his journey.[^p287-6]

January 14th. Fr. Kilian wrote to the Illustrious Bishop of Beijing, including another letter for Fr. Frossoloni,[^p287-7] open so that His Lordship could read it if he wished and, if he thought it appropriate, could hand it on to the Father. The reason for writing was that the Christians of Beijing, most anxious about what had been done regarding Europeans and Chinese, were discussing much among themselves what had happened from the first day of the arrival of the Lord Patriarch regarding the attack on the Chinese Rites. They attributed blame for the errors chiefly to Fathers Appiani and Frossoloni, whose language skills and services the Lord Patriarch used in these matters. Turning over in their minds what had happened, there were not a few who had bitter feelings against these interpreters, and therefore Fr. Kilian advised that if Fr. Frossoloni were to ask for the piao, it would be better if he sought it from the Emperor when he was passing through the city of Linqing, rather than come to Beijing, and expose himself to the feelings of certain of the Christians. | The Father indirectly hinted <!-- PDF page 288 | book page 278 --> to the His Lordship that he could also relieve himself from the labour of a winter journey, and wait in his own house for the Emperor to pass. It was now agreed in the court that he would be setting out for there in a month’s time. This letter of the Father you will find under January 20th.

January 17th. A letter was brought from the Illustrious Bishop of Beijing, which replied to the circular letter of the Father Superior J.F. Gerbillon. In this he declared that he had never followed the opinions of the Bishop of Conon and that we could produce this in the presence of the Emperor. At the same time, he asked us to seek permission that he would not be obliged to set out for Beijing.

[January] 18th. Because of the letter received yesterday, we did not expect the Illustrious Bishop of Beijing. But today he suddenly appeared at the door of our North Church with his companion, Reverend Father Carlo da Castorano of the Order of St. Francis. We received both with pleasure, hoping that we could draw some comfort in our affliction from their presence. The reason why the sudden resolution of coming to Beijing did not agree at all points with his previous letter was, His Lordship stated, that despite his wish, if possible, not to approach the Court in person, he had nevertheless been moved by a letter from Dr. Giovanni Borghese asking and imploring that for the common good he should not avoid this journey. The Doctor himself freely confessed that he had written to this effect and supplied us with a copy of his letter, which I here append.[^p288-8]

### 3. The letter of Dr. Borghese to the Bishop of Beijing. He narrates what had happened in the month of December

Most Illustrious Lord, On Sunday December 19th there appeared finally the edict of the Emperor expelling from the whole of China missionaries who did not have the imperial permit. Anyone who wants to receive the said permit must come to Beijing to get it. I believe it will be given only to those who oblige themselves to follow the decrees of the Emperor. But I am not certain and do not know whether it will be convenient for Your Illustrious self to come to Beijing to seek the said permit, in order not to abandon your flock without adequate reason; all the more since coming to Beijing cannot incur a greater punishment than exile from China, and by not coming one is already exiled. If you cannot see your way clear to come personally, I suppose you could send one of your Religious to find out what lies behind the edict. Furthermore it would appear that all the other missionaries will observe what Your Illustrious Self does as their

<!-- PDF page 289 | book page 279 | manuscript page 501 -->

Superior and one closer to Beijing, and that all will follow your example, and will be irresolute until they see what Your Illustrious self does. Monsignor de Conon, Signor Mezzafalce, and Monsieur Guéty have gone to Guangzhou with orders to the Governor that they should immediately be thrown out of China. Probably they are bound for Macau, and they left Beijing on the 21st of the current month. Signor Appiani has been sent in chains to the province of Sichuan with the order to examine his past life, and if he is cleared he will be exiled with the others. There left the same day the xianggong of Monsignor the Patriarch,[^p289-9] Peter Wang and Thomas Gu, who on the 23rd of the current month received 40 strokes and yesterday the 24th left for their exile in the province of Liaodong in a cart with a corporal and eight guards. I ask Your Illustrious self to give ten taels to Signor Carvalho, so that he can give them to the wife of the xianggong when he returns to the Church of Signor Mezzafalce. I promise and bind myself to repay them at your request.

I must also inform you that when Signor Appiani was imprisoned, the Lord Patriarch and his household were also arrested with orders not to leave that place without a new order. | I believe that they may be either eight or ten miles before arriving at Qingjiang wei,[^p289-10] which is a few days’ journey from Nanjing. I do not know whether he will later be sent on somewhere else. This conjecture is not without foundation.

Beijing December 25th.

Thus wrote Doctor Borghese, whose erroneous opinions we will not try to correct in this place.

### 4. The arrival of the Bishop of Conon in Beijing, the cause of the calamity

January 19th. Fr. Soares paid a courtesy call on the Most Illustrious Lord. Present also were Fathers Gerbillon and Jean Régis. When the conversation turned to how much harm had been done to the mission through the arrival of Bishop of Conon and the obstinacy of the Lord Patriarch in defending him, the Most Illustrious Lord said: “As soon as Father Appiani had informed me how he at the baths had informed the Mandarin Henkama (and consequently through him the Emperor) of the proximate arrival of the Bishop of Conon, I told him that he had made a stupid mistake. He excused himself on the ground that he did it at the order of Lord Patriarch. But I replied that in such a case <!-- PDF page 290 | book page 280 --> he should not have obeyed, knowing better than the Lord Patriarch how much harm would come to the mission.” These very words the same Most Illustrious Lord in the evening of that day distinctly repeated in the room of the superior J.F. Gerbillon, in the presence of Fr. Carlo da Castorano.

January 20th. Pursama had returned as anticipated from the hunting camp, with the news that Fr. Bouvet could have an audience with the Emperor when he arrived in Beijing, which would be soon. He had informed His Majesty of the opposition of Father Sabino, which he had seen with his own eyes at Guangzhou. He had also excused the delay in his return through the miseries of Father Sabino, who either because of illness or some other cause refused to ride and wanted to be carried in a chair for easily 300 leagues.

Today, Fr. Carlo da Castorano paid a courtesy call to the room of Fr. Kilian. Fr. Kilian expressed his sorrow that his letter of the 14th did not have the effect of relieving him of such a hard journey, of which there had not been need. Certainly Fr. Castorano had suffered very much on the journey and was quite weak when he arrived at Beijing. Fr. Kilian, hearing that Fr. Frossoloni sent him greetings, brought out a copy of his previously mentioned letter, which he had kept and read it out. See a copy of the letter at the end of this day’s entry. Fr. Castorano wondered who was the Christian mentioned in the letter. He freely admitted that Fr. Frossoloni had assisted with him at a nuptial mass in the house of a Christian, and he asserted that this had happened twice. Also, Fr. Frossoloni had ordered the Christians to bring him the tablets of their dead. Two had brought them, but one excused himself on the ground that the tablet was not in his house; the other, that relatives had taken the tablet elsewhere. In the same conversation, Fr. da Castorano told Fr. Kilian that in March of the previous year Fr. Frossoloni had written to him in Linqing, to order the tablets of the dead to be brought by every Christian to the Church there. This had been ordered by His Excellency the Lord Patriarch. But he had not dared make a change in a house or a church that did not belong to him without the prior authorization of the Lord Bishop; he had been ordered subsequently by the Bishop to suspend the execution of the order.

But since doubt seemed to remain whether any Christian had acted as Fr. Kilian had written in his letter to Fr. Frossoloni, on the following day Fr. Kilian brought this very Christian before Fr. da Castorano so that he could speak to him. He was John Huang,[^p290-11] a man over 50 known both to us and Fr. da Castorano.

### 5. Letter of Father Kilian Stumpf to Father Frossoloni[^p291-12] warning him not to come to Beijing

Most Reverend Father in Christ,

In the charity of Jesus Christ, and in the sincere union of our hearts, I wish to inform you of something which I consider to be of greatest importance to Your Reverence. A certain Christian from the household of a Prince, a duoluo qinwang,[^p291-13] the manager of his estate, has informed the Christians here that Your Paternity, when he had returned from Beijing to Linqing last Summer, forced the Christians to bring the tablets of the dead to the Church to be left there for Your Paternity to dispose of them. Another Christian, when he returned from Linqing, reported that after Your Paternity had come back from Beijing, you received a husband and wife in the front of the Church with ceremonies <!-- PDF page 292 | book page 282 --> and a Mass prescribed in the Ritual,[^p292-14] and called this rite sheng shen 聖身[^p292-15] or blessing of the bodies.

Whether this testimony of these two Christians is true or false I do not presume to judge, but leaving aside everything else, I intend one thing only, that Your Paternity should know what is being rumoured. Likewise, even if both reports are true, I in no way intend to judge whether what Your Reverend Paternity might have done was good or bad. I leave that to Your Paternity and the superiors of the mission to work out.

What good can come to me, and what good to Your Reverence, from this sincere account of mine? As for myself, I do not hope for any advantage whatsoever. For I can scarcely think of anything else to say than what Your Reverence said to me with his habitual bravery in adversity, last year on March 22nd when we were dealing with the petition of the Christians trampled underfoot by the Illustrious Patriarch, namely: “The less I think about the dangers, the less will they disturb me.” So be it: by this reply may I fulfill my duty to Your Reverence. I will not take it amiss. No one however will deny that have I fulfilled the duties of a sincere friend when I mentioned those things, which might be as a warning against the harm which, if it does not touch the person of Your Reverence, concerns however the mission, which Your Reverence cannot without blame wish destroyed.

Reverend Father, we in Beijing can speak in the presence of the Emperor on behalf of all missionaries, even our adversaries, for the common good, but we cannot deny facts. Each one must render an account, why he did this or that: and if he has offended against the laws of the Kingdom, there is no longer place for our entreaties. This is shown by the sad example of the Illustrious Bishop of Conon, and the Reverend Fathers Mezzafalce and Guéty.

### 6. Why Father Appiani was taken away to the province of Sichuan

And I don’t have to remind you of the case of Father Appiani, who was taken away bound in chains to Sichuan in order to undergo an examination to be carried out by the mandarins who had the obligation of reporting to the Emperor to await his sentence.

There is a common imposition on missionaries that, if they wished to remain in China, they should ask immediately for a permit from the Emperor. But he <!-- PDF page 293 | book page 283 | manuscript page 503 --> will give this in accordance with the information which he gains about each one himself or receives from others. Therefore it will not be unwelcome to Your Paternity to know beforehand something which will make it more difficult to come to seek such a permit. I believe that it is by far more expedient that Your Reverence should desist from a journey to see the Emperor, and not expose yourself to the Christians of Beijing, who for several months have been anxious over what Your Reverence, like Father Appiani, presumed to do or say at the Court.

The Emperor is now in Tartary, near the Great Wall; he will return from there before the 20th of this month, and around the 20th of the first month of the year will be going to the Nanbian.[^p293-16] There a convenient opportunity will be given Your Reverence to appear before the Emperor. The Jesuits too, if the journey of the Emperor becomes certain, | will use the same occasion.

### 7. Why the Emperor conceded some delay in promulgating his decree throughout the provinces

Others from the North are expected in Beijing before the (Chinese) New Year. Already Father Van Hamme and Father Resende have received their permits, nor is it allowable to delay the application for a permit. It was only with difficulty that we obtained from the Emperor that he would not publish his edict in the provinces before the missionaries had been informed through private letters that they should present themselves to His Majesty. And although the Emperor has suspended his edict through thirteen provinces, he ordered that it should be communicated to the mandarins in Guangdong and Sichuan so that those who do not have permits will not be allowed to reside in either place. Those three reverend gentlemen will be expelled to Macau and never allowed to return. The distinction between the missionaries in Guangdong and Sichuan and the missionaries of other provinces is this, that the mandarins are not to harm the latter, and ought to allow them time to consider, so that they might freely choose either to approach the Emperor, or, if they wished, to depart from China. The mandarins can order the latter to depart or prescribe a way of coming to the Emperor. By a strict order, the mandarins who are the guards of the Illustrious Bishop of Conon and Father Appiani can do what they wish with them both. Our missionaries in Guangzhou will undergo the same treatment as the others without our being able to offer them any other remedy, except that we have informed them <!-- PDF page 294 | book page 284 --> through the above mentioned couriers that they should as soon as possible undertake the journey to meet the Emperor on his way, or see him in Beijing before the sixth month. In this way, they will escape the hands of the mandarins if they set out before the order has reached Guangzhou. This, Reverend Father, is my simple and candid opinion, which I am writing because our superior, Antoine Thomas, is away on missionary business, nor do I know if there is any other Father who would dare so much as I, because of our long friendship, presume to do.

There is no need to acknowledge this letter, nor to reply to it. It will be enough if you understand the concern of a friend and pardon what I write in confidence. I commend myself to the most holy sacrifices of Your Reverence.

14 January, 1707.

Your Servant in Christ,

Kilian Stumpf.

This letter was received and opened by Reverend Father Frossoloni. He himself subsequently acknowledged as much in the presence of Father Franchi.[^p294-17] The Lord Bishop of Beijing on the 18th said he knew about it, and had left permission at his house that Fr. Frossoloni should open all letters even those directed to his Sacred Person.

January 21st. The Most Illustrious Bishop of Beijing with his companion were announced in the Palace by the Father Superior J.F. Gerbillon who declared he was a man who had lived peacefully in China for many years, and who enjoyed a good reputation both among all the Europeans and the Chinese. In the evening, Fr. Giampaolo Gozani, SJ came from the city of Kaifeng in the province of Henan, to present himself to receive the piao.

### 8. The Bishop of Beijing is rebuked by the Prince as a patron of the Lord Patriarch

January 22nd. The Bishop of Beijing, Fr. da Castorano, and Fr. Gozani, who were about to request the piao, were first led into the Palace and the presence of the Prince. The Most Illustrious Lord was recognized by the mandarins who asked: why was he here now wanting the piao, when he had for so many months sat at the side of Duoluo, the Lord Patriarch, and had to share his blame because he had either helped him with advice or at least had not distanced himself from what the Lord Patriarch had wrongly done? Hearing this, the Prince seriously threatened that he would expel the Bishop from China. Fr.

<!-- PDF page 295 | book page 285 | manuscript page 504 -->

J.F. Gerbillon intervened quietly, excusing him on the grounds that the vehement natural disposition of the Lord Patriarch could not be controlled by the Lord Bishop. Even though he had continually suggested milder measures, it did not shake his resolve or persuade him to a reasonable manner of acting. The Prince made no reply except that the piao would be given to the other two.

### 9. Nine conditions proposed for reception of the or royal permit

Not long afterwards three mandarins | advised us that those requesting the piao should each write in his own hand his name, age, country, length of stay in China, his piao wish to remain in China, and opinion regarding the view of either the Bishop of Conon or Matteo Ricci. This was the first time such a rule was prescribed for those who were to accept the piao. For the two previous applicants, Fathers Van Hamme and Resende had only orally replied to the questions.

January 23rd. The Most Illustrious Lord Bishop with the said Fathers presented the document demanded and, having seen it, the Emperor promised them the piao. The Prince again said that such a permit should not be given to the Most Illustrious Lord because he belonged to the faction of the Lord Patriarch. Father Superior J.F. Gerbillon repeated what he had said the previous day, adding even more in order to destroy the suspicion in the mind of the Prince. Then the Prince, persuaded by this plea, promised the piao also to the Lord Bishop if things were as the Father had said, but did not go as far as executing it. The Father in vain urged that the diploma should be quickly granted, fearing lest further delay should give an opportunity for the changeable will of the Prince to shift to the contrary. Delay undoubtedly was necessary because on the next day the seals of all the Tribunals would be locked up for the beginning of the New Year holidays.

January 24th. Fr. Bouvet was ordered by the Palace to hand over a list of names of the Europeans and Chinese who had come in the company of Fr. Sabino. Such an order provided the opportunity for a young man of Siamese or some such nationality who spoke French to say that he had served the reverend clerics[^p295-18] in the Kingdom of Siam and was said to know how to mend clocks, and he had been taken on by Fr. Sabino as a servant. Fr. Sabino then took him with him to Guangzhou.

January 25th. The Illustrious Bishop of Beijing, with the often-mentioned Fathers, went to the Palace and today for the first time was allowed to enter the inner door, which is called the Dragon Door.[^p295-19] He did not receive the piao, <!-- PDF page 296 | book page 286 | manuscript page 505 --> and awaited, with patience on his part and sadness on ours, the benevolence of the Prince and the Emperor.

### 10. A statement of the Lord Patriarch uttered in anger which offended the Emperor

It happened in the evening, that Fr. da Castorano was talking with the Jesuits and heard of the first error so harmful to the affairs of the Lord Patriarch, when the latter, burning with anger and preoccupied in mind, burst out to the mandarins that the Emperor, yielding to the suggestions of others, had broken his promises.[^p296-20] It was said that the Emperor, in the Chinese manner graciously ignoring how offended he felt, had sent the mandarins to the Lord Patriarch, to ask pardon, and they had filled the house with their clamours in breach of the rules of modesty.[^p296-21] This was to teach the Lord Patriarch what he should have offered of his own accord, and so that His Majesty might learn whether, after he had recovered from his anger, realizing his error, he would take care to amend. Father da Castorano, extending his arms to heaven, exclaimed: “Oh, God! The Lord Patriarch took this to be his own justification, as if the Emperor himself was acknowledging that he had lightly given ear to others; that this went to prove that he had grounds for complaint that men with ill intentions had persuaded the Emperor to change his mind. One must wonder first why the Lord Patriarch did not refrain from making remarks of this kind in the presence of prudent men; secondly, why others believed what he said when they knew that his Chinese Majesty should not be humiliated before anyone. They should have perceived the manifest falsity of the argument, because the Emperor who is said to deplore lightness in changing one’s opinion, could not be said to concede, nor had ever conceded requests, even after the most humble supplication, which he had once publicly and deliberately refused to grant.”

January 26th. A question arose concerning the slow progress of the journey of the Lord Patriarch. The brother of the Governor of Guangdong asked Father J.F. Gerbillon for the reason. He replied the reason was said to be shortage of the money | which the conducting officials (servants of the Viceroy and zongdu) furnished. The mandarin brother of the Viceroy declared that this was quite untrue, saying that he had given to his servants a thousand Chinese ounces of silver as money for the journey, and the same amount was given to others from the household of the zongdu by the zongdu’s son.

### 11. The Bishop of Beijing is admitted to the Emperor and dismissed kindly

January 28th. Today, finally, after he had gone so many times to the Palace, the Illustrious Bishop of Beijing with two companions was admitted into the presence of the Emperor. Leading them were Fathers Tomás Pereira and Dominique Parrenin. While they stood in order before him, the Emperor asked the Prince which of them had been in the company of the Lord Patriarch. “The older man,” replied the Prince. He asked further whether he knew Chinese characters. The Prince replied: “He knows neither the language nor characters.” He added immediately that Fr. Gerbillon said that he was a lover of peace and that he did not support the Lord Patriarch, let alone agree with him, but rather tried to dissuade him from his erroneous opinion and manner of acting.

When they were dismissed from the sight of His Majesty, the Prince absolutely refused that the permit should be given to the Lord Bishop, and said that nothing remained for him but to return to his native country and live out his years there. The Fathers disagreed and spoke on behalf of the permit being given. The Prince resisted, saying that he was the very one whom the Lord Patriarch was accustomed to call immediately every time business was completed and he had sent away the mandarin negotiators. In view of this obstinacy on the part of the Prince, all the Fathers present fell on their knees seven or eight times, and tried with gentle words to soften his recalcitrant position. But the Prince treated it all as a joke and sent His Lordship of Beijing away empty handed.

January 29th. A slight hope remained yesterday of obtaining the diploma for the Lord Bishop in that, although the Prince constantly refused it, he never said it was prohibited by the Emperor himself. This slight hope, I say, was today to some extent revived by Zhao Laoye who argued from the fact that there had been no positive prohibition.

Father Sabino was called early in the morning and led to the Palace by the Sub-prefect from the Wuying dian.[^p297-22] There he remained a long time and, in his opinion, more than required. In the morning, according to his custom, he had had a drink of chocolate, but as midday was approaching, he was unable to bear the emptiness of his stomach, and wanted his dinner brought to the Palace. He complained in not a few words of the affliction brought on by hunger. Father Parrenin, acting as interpreter, was able to offer no other solace to console the sufferer except the example of our Fathers who suffered similar <!-- PDF page 298 | book page 288 | manuscript page 506 --> hunger, not once as in his case, but almost daily and put up with it in silence so that from our privations it may be well with missionaries throughout the provinces. Nevertheless we are accused of either not fasting ourselves or not teaching the obligation to fast.[^p298-23]

### 12. Father Sabino is questioned by the Prince

January 30th. For the second time Father Sabino was led to the Palace accompanied by Father Grimaldi, and, as yesterday, was ordered to wait outside the Dragon Gate. Then, in the place called the Qingyin gong,[^p298-24] the Prince proposed the following questions to him, Father Dominique Parrenin acting as interpreter for both these questions and the replies of Father Sabino.

Q.1: Yan Dang (the Bishop of Conon) came to me in this Hall (put forward by the Lord Patriarch) and said many stupid things. Fang Zhou (Father Guéty) threw a letter into the toilet and denied he had received (the letter) and told lie after lie. Duoluo (the Lord Patriarch) made errors in the presence of the Emperor in many matters, taking falsehood for truth and truth for falsehood, and offended the Emperor. From this one can see that you of the Order of St. Peter[^p298-25] have not come to preach but to destroy your Law. What do you say to that? Reply: I have come from the city of Rome, which is the City of Peace; I have come to bring peace, not war: I do not know what those three have done; I was in Guangzhou and they have not informed me; if I had been present, I would have exhorted them that they should obey the Emperor, as far as their consciences allowed.

Q.2: Did you come | to China to follow the Lord Patriarch and to return with him, or to remain in China? Reply: I was chosen by the Pope to come to China independently of the Lord Patriarch; even if he had not come, I would have come; but because he was coming, I came with him.

Q.3: Have you proof that you were chosen by the Pope? Reply: No, the Sacred Congregation simply told the Pope that I wanted to come to China, and the Pope agreed and later saw me.

<!-- PDF page 299 | book page 289 -->

Q.4: Do you want to remain in China, and for what? Reply: to spread the Law.

Q.5: What Law? Reply: The Christian Law.

Q.6: Do you want to spread this Law as Yan Dang (the Bishop of Conon) spread it, or as Li Madou (Father Ricci) spread it? Reply: There are not two Gospels nor two Christian Laws. So I came to spread the Gospel.

Q.7: But do you want to do it according to the method used by Yan Dang (the Bishop of Conon) or that used by Li Madou? Reply: I do not know what they did. I am a new arrival.

Q.8: If you don’t know, let me tell you. Li Madou (Father Ricci) and others were in China under two dynasties; they lived in peace, they were loved and honoured by everybody, and took care of the Chinese. Yan Dang (the Bishop of Conon) came to China only a few years ago and because, in the province of Fujian he wished to take away the royal tablet and to forbid the use of Jing Tian, he drove his followers to pull his beard and kick him with their feet.[^p299-26] Is this the method you think should be followed? Reply: I do not know the language, the characters, the customs of the Chinese, nor anything about these matters.

### 13. He replies to the questions asked

Q.9: If nonetheless you wish to remain in China, which of these two will you follow? Reply: I will follow the majority.

Q.10: Where is the majority, in the followers of Yan Dang or the followers of Li Madou? Reply: I do not know.

Q.11: Are you deaf as well as blind? If you didn’t see, at least you must have seen something. Reply: No. I was in Guangzhou; nothing was written to me about these matters as Father Bouvet knows.

<!-- PDF page 300 | book page 290 | manuscript page 507 -->

Q.12: Wasn’t anything at all written to you from Beijing by the Lord Patriarch? Reply: I received one letter from the Lord Patriarch after my departure, when things were still good and afterwards nothing was written to me.

Q.13: But the Lord Patriarch put forward the Bishop of Conon as a learned man, although he was skilled in neither our language nor our letters; it is also said that the Bishop of Conon came here sent by the Pope to examine our teaching, but how could he examine it being ignorant of our language and characters? Reply: I do not know what the Bishop of Conon knows or does not know. The Emperor knows better than I. If I had been here, I would have exhorted all to obey the Emperor so far as conscience allowed.

Q.14: How could you do this, you who disputed on the journey with Fr. Bouvet wishing to take precedence over him despite him being the Emperor’s man, on the grounds that you had been sent by the Lord Patriarch? Reply: In what did I dispute? In what did I not yield? If this is said about the gifts, it is true that I did not hand them over to him because they had been given to me in Beijing. But many times on the journey I said to the Father: “My Father, let us go together, let us offer the gifts to the Holy Pontiff together.”

Q.15: But why were you unwilling to believe our Pursai (or Purgama), when he was sent to Guangzhou, to bring back the gifts and you, and to seek proof from him? Did you not recognize the very man who first conducted you to Guangzhou, and now was recalling you? Reply: I knew him: nor did I deny him the gifts, since I knew very well that I would be loaded with chains, if I did not hand over the gifts. But Pursai wrongly suspected that I was unwilling to hand over the gifts.

Q.16: But why were you unwilling to believe Fr. Bouvet when he interpreted for you the royal decree, but wanted other interpreters. Reply: It was Fr. Bouvet who called Fr. Tarin. I am not so stupid as to wish to hold back the gifts, since I could not eat them, nor convert them to my use.

Q.17: But when you came with the Lord Patriarch, did you know nothing of the Lord Patriarch’s business. Reply: I knew nothing. I spoke with him on the ship, | but he did not reveal to me the secrets of his heart.

Q.18: Fang Zhou (Father Guéty) says that the Fathers of the Society of Jesus falsely attribute to themselves the name of Society of Jesus and should be called the Society of St. Ignatius. Reply: Everywhere they are called Fathers of the Society of Jesus.

<!-- PDF page 301 | book page 291 -->

Q.19: Do you wish to abolish all the Chinese customs and change them for European customs. Reply: No. For in foreign kingdoms one should not easily change customs unless they are bad.

These were most of the questions put by the Prince at the order of the Emperor, with the assistance of the mandarins, and the replies given by Father Sabino on bended knees outside the door of the pavilion[^p301-27] in which the Prince sat.[^p301-28]

Today the Illustrious Bishop of Beijing proposed in the name of Dr. Borghese, the physician to Father Superior Gerbillon, that we should petition the Emperor that if he did not wish to use his services, he should be dismissed in peace. The medical doctor desired to accompany Father Sabino in his journey; he was already tired of waiting around at the Court. From the beginning of November 1706 until the second last day of January, only one sick person had been committed to his care.[^p301-29] The response was that we could not procure the departure of this gentleman unless he himself went to the Palace to ask for it. We had indeed many and grave reasons for desiring that he should depart if possible of his own accord. For we knew that he was looking for matters which he could put in his diary to our disadvantage, and that he had already written a number of the most false and serious accusations, with which perhaps we will deal in an appendix.[^p301-30] The Most Illustrious Bishop on this occasion declared that the medical doctor wished to depart, but chose to go in such a way that no one could say he did so through his own initiative. This was so that he might attribute it to the Jesuits.

### 14. The exile of Father Sabino is ordered by the Emperor

January 31st. As yesterday and the third day, so today the Fathers asked in the Palace for the permit to be given to the Illustrious Bishop of Beijing. Regarding its success, the Mandarin Zhao told them: “Recently I suggested there was some hope, <!-- PDF page 302 | book page 292 | manuscript page 508 --> but now I have given up all hope. The reason is that when the Emperor asked today whether the Europeans still persist in making the request and the Prince replied that they did persist, he laughed at them for doing so. His Majesty immediately as if in disdain added: ‘What a fuss these Europeans make with their petitions!’ From this I gather from now on there can be no more proposals of this kind.” Furthermore, Zhao told us that yesterday’s replies of Father Sabino had been related and that when the Emperor heard them he said Nian ta ba![^p302-31] “Let him be expelled! It is enough!”

Before I conclude the events of January, it will be appropriate to add a few matters which happened in Nanjing, and without which the events in Beijing which followed cannot be fully understood. I will state them briefly, either as they were expressed in letters or as authentic reports by Rev. Fr. Antonio da Silva, Vicar Apostolic of Nanjing.

The Lord Patriarch had received in Nanjing the aforementioned letter of our Father Superior J.F. Gerbillon sent on the 23rd day of October 1706, with the enclosed decree of the Emperor concerning the expulsion of the Bishop of Conon etc. and concerning the royal diploma to be requested by all missionaries. In reply His Excellency wrote a letter dated 18 January 1707, bitter and full of calumnies, and on the outside he had written contrary to his custom[^p302-32] the following: “To Rev. Father Jean François Gerbillon, Superior of the French members of the Society of Jesus, and to the other Fathers of the same Society living in the Beijing Court, Beijing.”

### 15. Letter of the Lord Patriarch, full of complaints and anger against the Fathers[^p302-33]

Text of the letter: I have recently received from Your Reverence a letter full of lamentation, together with the enclosed imperial decree given the 17th of December 1706 against the Lord Bishop of Conon and others. You have had a victory | twice over. You have prevented Truth from triumphing in China and added not only its supporters but <!-- PDF page 303 | book page 293 --> its witnesses. But you say you are sorry! Would that the sorrow of Your Reverences lead to repentance. I would certainly rejoice if that were the case, because it would be according to God and would work towards a sure salvation.

I am indeed no less afflicted over the affairs of the mission than over those that afflict it, and I pour out tears to God most of the night. If I did not know the cause of the evils, and their authors, I would bear them more cheerfully. Your practice has been condemned by the Holy See,[^p303-34] but more to be detested is the immoderate manner of acting, by which you try to bury your shame with the destruction of the mission. You have not given a hearing to sane counsel, but have fled together to things that make my hair stand on end.

What can I say? Alas! The case is finished, but not yet the error. The mission must be destroyed before it can be reformed. But Your Reverences still play games: you do not grieve when you say that the Emperor is angry with you, yet he has decided everything according to your wishes. In fact, the Emperor would have been provoked to just anger if he knew (may God avert it) how much harm you have brought to his glory. True zeal for religion is shown not by spouting words but by solid works of virtue. Why should I believe those who always laid traps in their dealings with me, who on the very day so many burdens were laid on me in my apostolic administrations, pretend to petition me on behalf of a catechist?[^p303-35] I ask Him, who has reserved punishment to Himself, that he may not give you the retribution your deeds deserve, nor measure out to you, the measure you have meted out to your neighbours. Many of these things, which follow one another here every day, were already predicted in Rome as consequences of the non-publication of the decision in Europe.[^p303-36] Who is slow to judge judges more justly, but holds the more firmly to the judgment reached.

<!-- PDF page 304 | book page 294 -->

You pour out mournful complaints on all sides against the brutality of the way in which all your work is now treated, but you should turn your hands to your consciences. If you knew that man so well that he deserved to be called by you Herod, why have you had recourse to him?[^p304-37] Why have you appealed on religious matters to our adversaries? Why have you wickedly aroused his anger against an apostolic legate to the extent of averting him from his duties? Your Reverences should consider your deeds, and you will be able to complain about nobody but yourselves. Would that you grieve with all your hearts.

Given at Nanjing, 18 January 1707.

Most devoted to Your Reverences,

Charles Thomas, Patriarch of Antioch

To Fr. Gerbillon, Superior of the French Fathers of the Society for all the Fathers of the Society of Jesus at the Court of Beijing.

To this letter on the same day was added another, mild in tone and leaving aside all bitterness and bile, to Father Gerbillon, complaining only that in the formula of the permit something honorific should be added. Father da Silva however wrote in his relation[^p304-38] as follows: On the 18th of January, the Lord Patriarch sent an invitation through a messenger to Father Vice-Provincial José Monteiro in Zhenjiang.[^p304-39] The Father Vice-Provincial had set out before the messenger arrived. His Excellency himself invited Father Vice-Provincial and myself the same day to enter his house and gave a banquet for everybody at which he publicly acknowledged us.

From this I gather that Lord Patriarch did not either reveal to the Fathers the above-mentioned letter, or what he was cooking in his mind, lest he might render sweet food bitter.

He produced his regulation or decree[^p304-40] on the 25th, so he must have written it on the preceding days, during which he had with him only recent arrivals who were still ignorant of the Chinese language.

### 16. The Lord Patriarch proposes a Jubilee at Nanjing

At the end of January (writes the Most Rev. Fr. da Silva) the Lord Patriarch told me that he intended to proclaim a Jubilee,[^p305-41] and that he would order it to be carried out in all the residences of this diocese in order that Our Lord God would preserve the mission, which stood in danger. It was printed and published in Nanjing, granting 20 days in which to gain it, the first ten days for men, the second ten days for women. At the end of the Jubilee for the men, he confirmed 120 Christians. The women, too, had been prepared but on the evening before the Sunday at the end of their ten days, when they were to be confirmed as had been agreed to by the Patriarch, the same Patriarch ordered me to say that he would not confirm the women because he feared a disturbance if the gentiles came to know about it. He said this was the reason he had decided in the end not to confirm them, but this advice of the Lord Patriarch contradicted what he had said previously.[^p305-42]

During the whole of this time the main activity of the Lord Patriarch was to lament that he was caught between two strong wills in the matter of the Chinese Rites, that of the Holy Pontiff, and that of the Emperor of China. His secretary Angelita told me on several occasions that the decree of the reigning Holy Pontiff, Clement XI, prohibiting the Rites, was in the hands of the Lord Patriarch. But, despite this, at this time the same Lord Patriarch said nothing about it to Father Vice-Provincial José Monteiro, nor to me; to me in particular <!-- PDF page 306 | book page 296 --> he said that the same decree was already prepared when Pope Innocent XII died.[^p306-43] This is what was printed in the Apology of the Dominicans.[^p306-44] So the Lord Patriarch was preparing his forces by making battle plans. The main one was the following. On January 28th the Lord Patriarch wrote a letter to Father Provincial in which he said the following:

### 17. Another letter of the Patriarch[^p306-45]

Charles Thomas Patriarch of Antioch, Apostolic Visitor with the faculty of Legate a Latere, etc.

To the Reverend Father in Christ, José Monteiro of the Society of Jesus, Vice- Provincial of the Vice-Province of China, greetings etc.

I wonder at a new and hardly Religious way of acting by which some relying on their power conduct the most grave affairs of the mission with such authority and independence as if we were not in China. We keep silence indeed so that with patience if possible, with the help of God, we may conquer. Nevertheless we in no way consent to the disordered activities of those men. From their hasty and disobedient actions the state of mind of those acting is revealed. They hurry to take refuge in confessing that they deserve punishment, which they avoid by pleading ill health to the great detriment of their reputation. But we leave these matters to be considered by others.

### 18. He condemns and forbids the promise made by missionaries to remain perpetually in China

For the present only this must be noted, that those who absolutely promise things which depend on the will of another, without asking his consent even when they can, no less deceive those with whose knowledge the obligations are undertaken than they harm the rights of those whom they negligently pass by. Of such a kind is the promise made by some without consulting us of remaining perpetually in China, which since it is not strengthened by the consent of their Superiors[^p306-46] is of no moment and validity, <!-- PDF page 307 | book page 297 | manuscript page 510 --> and is more in the nature of a legal deceit than of obedience to the Emperor. Do we blush to speak to Superiors and to show them the due signs of reverence in the presence of the pagans? Very often, indeed. These things however cannot but be heard with disapproval by any man of probity. Indeed the Chinese, not without the most grave scandal and with contempt for Christianity, see what we fail to do, since not in books alone but also in the manner of their life they make profession of complete obedience. So, therefore, that things may be rightly and validly done, and that all may be mindful of their obedience due to the Holy See and its legate, we are forced to declare, as we have declared, and declare | in the terms of the present statutes, that all who have bound themselves to remain perpetually in China without any previous written permission from us are suspended ipso facto from the work of a missionary or of a priest. Your Reverence, of whose moderation of mind we have no doubt, will not find it difficult to make this order issued by virtue of our office and in our name known to each and every Father of the Society of Jesus of this mission, even the French. In the meantime we ask God that he may long keep you unharmed.

Given at Nanjing from our House at the Church of St. Joseph, 28 January 1707. Seal (in the form of a cross). Charles Thomas, Patriarch of Antioch etc.

Andreas Candela, Chancellor of the Apostolic Visitation and Apostolic Missionary. [Acts of the Apostolic Visitation. Fol. 84][^p307-47]

The Father Vice-Provincial after reading this letter replied: 1. that he could not take to himself responsibility for the French Fathers because they had nothing do with him, and they had their own French superior; and 2. he replied in the following letter.[^p307-48]

However I postpone this to February which we begin immediately after this fourfold note. Please note:

1. That, according to the dates on the letters, between this letter of the Lord Patriarch and that to the Fathers of Beijing there intervened ten days. 2. That this was given to the Portuguese Superior to be communicated to the French, and that sent to the Superior of the French it was to be communicated to the Portuguese, perhaps so that neither party would have a pretext for saying it <!-- PDF page 308 | book page 298 --> did not apply to them. 3. That this was written at Nanjing on January 28th and handed in Nanjing to the assistant to the Vice-Provincial José Monteiro on February 1st. 4. That this was given to the Father to be sent around at that time of the Chinese year when the couriers and all the government offices were closed for almost an entire month. Moreover His Excellency the Lord Patriarch was advised about this and had already learnt this Chinese practice the preceding year.
