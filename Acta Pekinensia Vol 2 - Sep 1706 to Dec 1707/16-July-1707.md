### 1. Father Baborier, why he was proscribed by public edict, and what was the outcome

On July 5th the Emperor, in order to avoid the heat of Beijing, went away to Tartary, taking with him Father Dominique Parrenin and Brother Paramino.

On July 12th Fathers Domenge and Baborier set out to return home. The latter, when he arrived at his church in Hanzhong, found that he had already been proscribed in a public edict by the Prefect of the district. The Father complained of this injustice and, showing his residence permit, had the decree of unjust proscription repealed.

### 2. One of the Dominicans gives rise to a very serious source of trouble

The occasion of this so unjust decision of the mandarin was the fact that some officials from the province of Zhejiang had brought it about that the Rev. Fr. Tomás Cróquer, of the Order of St. Dominic, was being sought throughout the whole of China as a fugitive. For it had happened that the Reverend Dominican Fathers, ordered by the Emperor to depart, were on their way to Guangzhou, and for this, as they moved from place to place, they had to receive documents from the mandarins in which their names etc. were recorded. But it happened, I do not know for what reason, that the Rev. Fr. Tomás Cróquer went back one day’s journey. After the mandarins found out in the evening, at the place where they were to spend the night, that there was one member of the party missing, they wrote next day to all the neighbouring districts for some distance around, then also to the viceroys, and then to all the provinces, that this European was to be brought back from his flight. Since rumour grows as it spreads further, so this unfortunate inquisition gathered momentum, and more and more mandarins promulgated that more and more Europeans were to be sought, and among these Fathers Carvalho and Montigny, and Father Astudillo, who was called Ai in Chinese,[^p587-1] were especially mentioned. This caused no small trouble to Father Provana, who was called by the same Chinese name as the last.[^p587-2]

### 3. Old hatreds of the Chinese against the Europeans revive

These Fathers had not fled, nor had the Rev. Fr. Cróquer, but the Chinese, with malicious intent, spread the rumour in order to promote ill will against the Europeans. It will be difficult for many people across the western sea to grasp <!-- PDF page 588 | book page 578 | manuscript page 686 --> adequately this unfortunate happening and its harmful effects, since even in China itself missionaries who had been here for not a few years could scarcely understand, writing to us how greatly they wondered at the insolence of the Chinese, so that (they said) they seemed to want all Europeans to be expelled from their China. It was a source of astonishment to them that the complete peace, which they had enjoyed in tranquillity since the year 1692, was no longer granted to them in undisturbed possession in the way that they calmly thought it ought to be. It is indeed true, by an eternal decree of God, that everything that may promote their preaching is owed, both by Christians and by those rulers who live outside the Law of God, to men engaged in the apostolate. But it is equally an eternal law that Christ and the faith of Christ will be a sign of contradiction in this world, and that evil men will persecute those engaged in the apostolate, as the original apostles were persecuted. And although we missionaries, truly a small flock in China, should not fear those who can kill the body, nevertheless it is appropriate for us not to be surprised as we reflect on the fact that the Chinese persecute us foreigners, since Christ in the flesh was persecuted by those united to him by the same Holy Law, country, tribe, and blood. Moreover, this rumour alone concerning the flight of the Rev. Fr. Cróquer, although false, had such an effect that many churches were plundered by the gentiles. But I am entrusting the details of these ill effects to a special treatise and am returning to events in Beijing. |

### 4. The Mandarins set in motion a persecution against the Christians

On July 10th Father Kilian wrote to Father Miguel Fernández a letter which will be inserted below. The reason for writing was, as I have just said, that the mandarins, either indulging their own ill feeling or conniving with the insolence of the people, were everywhere committing all kinds of outrages against the Christians and their churches, and permitting such acts by others. Thus it was very dangerous for those who wished to preach against the Chinese rites according to the instructions of the Lord Patriarch to further stir up the people who were already enraged; it could happen that a great crowd of gentiles might destroy, in one murderous rush, the small flocks of Christ. Wiser Christians feared this very thing, and since Shandong Province borders on the province of Beijing, and the Christians of Shandong and those of Beijing are in constant communication with each other, even on a daily basis, the latter made known to the former what took place in the tribunals there concerning the search for the “fugitive” missionaries (as we have explained earlier), and what Fr. Fernández may have done in his church against the Chinese rites in such a difficult time.

### 5. Father Fernández, the Franciscan, indulging his zeal too much, brought the cause of religion into grave danger

Indeed the said Reverend Father had allowed himself to be carried away too much by his zeal. He had personally enrolled in the Third Order of St. Francis some common Chinese of the place. In their holy but ignorant simplicity, they wished to join him in taking away from others the rites to which they themselves were not at that time bound.[^p589-3] With their approval, and going further than them in his actions, the Reverend Father wholeheartedly urged that the regulation of the Lord Patriarch, translated by himself into Chinese and made public, be observed by all. The result was that there also now arose a doubt in the city of Ji’nan fu whether Christians could confess to Fr. Girolamo Franchi, SJ, who was a missionary in the same city and who had not yet promulgated the regulation of the Patriarch, because he had not yet been ordered to promulgate it by the Lord Bishop of Beijing. Before Father Kilian took up his pen to write, he warned Rev. Fr. Buonaventura da Roma about all these matters. The latter was still in Beijing, and he himself said that he knew that the ardour of the man was not at all appropriate for these times, and that on his return to the Most Illustrious Bishop of Beijing he would take care that he use his authority to insist upon moderation. The letter follows:

### 6. Father Kilian writes to him

Very Reverend Father, the peace of Christ be with you.

The friendship between us, which has been so strong up to now, did not develop and grow through ties of flesh and blood, but was founded on the love of God and the love of our neighbour, namely the whole Chinese nation. So it is right that what arose from such holy beginnings be maintained perpetually and permit the rights of friendship to be exercised without any detriment to the love of one another. Therefore I make bold to write to Your Most Reverend Paternity what I would not fear to say to a friend, and I am firmly persuaded that I am not seeking anything for myself, nor is Your Reverend Paternity seeking anything for yourself, but in each of us Christ is being sought, and in Christ each of us is seeking the salvation of souls.

For some time I have known, from Christians, that Your Reverend Paternity promulgated in your church on Palm Sunday the regulation of the Lord Patriarch, and that you affirmed on oath, in the presence of the neophytes, that you would observe it; this took place in the intermediate time, during which Your Paternity, a little beforehand, had professed in writing to the Emperor <!-- PDF page 590 | book page 580 | manuscript page 687 --> that you had hitherto held to another practice not contrary to the Chinese rites, and so were expecting an imperial document with the permission to remain in China. I did not altogether trust the report of the Christians, especially because | I did not hear anything of that nature from Fr. Franchi, who was living in the same city with Your Paternity. Further, I was beginning to wonder whether Your Reverend Paternity had been carried away by the promptness of your obedience and your ardent zeal, just as I was aware that some others, even among our missionaries, had been carried away. Setting aside a more careful consideration of the matter, they promptly put into effect measures which they did not doubt were harmful to the mission; but, wrongly persuading themselves that the damage could and ought to be averted by others, they devoted their spiritual strength to obeying blindly, as if the mission could without sin be ruined through obedience. Therefore, until this day, I have written nothing on this matter to Your Reverend Paternity, filled with the hope that, with the passage of time, through both the keenness of your own judgement and the example of others, you might recognise the danger both to yourself and to your neophytes, and correctly perceive what, according to the rules of the Catholic Church, the example of the saints in the primitive Church would suggest should be done concerning the present state of the Chinese mission.

But now, once again, the Christians are warning that Your Reverend Paternity is urging putting into effect the regulation decided upon by the Patriarch, and they desire that the danger threatening themselves as a result of this (according to the decrees of the Emperor) be averted; hence Your Reverend Paternity will not wonder at the audacity of my pen, which puts before another what true friendship in the Lord requires.

I do not at all see why Your Paternity is at this time pressing the regulation on the Christians, for in no time or place has the Lord Patriarch ordered it to be put before the Christians; this is clear from the general purport of the regulation, and also from the fact that no other instruction, either direct or indirect, has come from the Lord Patriarch imposing the promulgation of the said regulation to neophytes. What is more, if not in word then certainly in action, the author of the regulation himself has suspended it, and Your Reverend Paternity cannot be ignorant of this, since you know for a fact that the Lord Patriarch wrote to the Rev. Fr. da Castrocaro, Pro-Vicar Apostolic in Shaanxi, that he accept the imperial permit, with any condition whatsoever, so long as the regulation is not positively overthrown and the obedience of superiors concerning the perpetual residence in China is preserved. Also, since the same Most Reverend and

<!-- PDF page 591 | book page 581 | manuscript page 688 -->

Illustrious Lord Patriarch sent to Beijing from his side Rev. Fr. Buonaventura da Roma, a missionary of the Sacred Congregation, to receive the imperial permit on the same conditions under which it had been given to missionaries by the Emperor, he undoubtedly knew then about their journey, there being present the Reverend Fathers San Giorgio and Hervé and our Vice-Provincial with his four companions. From all of these also His Excellency would have known that eleven other missionaries had asked for the imperial permit with this condition. Among them was the Most Reverend Father da Silva, then Vicar Apostolic of Nanjing, who had before that been bound by oath to the regulation, and many others were to ask for it under the same condition. Nevertheless he did not rebuke them either by word or in writing, or put any obstacle in their way, as is clear to Your Reverend Paternity in your own case. Thus it was clear to the Lord Patriarch that his regulation had become impossible to implement, or not useful for the building up of the mission, and that it should not be pressed at the cost of the mission’s destruction.

If Your Most Reverend Paternity were to say that the Most Illustrious Lord Patriarch did this in the case of Father Buonaventura in order to save his houses, which had recently been bought, and that he had passed over the ejection accompanied by terrible threats of the death of the reverend clerics, the Dominican Fathers, and some Jesuits as simply a rain-storm—I do not know why you would say that, unless perhaps it seemed better to make concessions so that the houses acquired with Roman money not be lost rather than that the Chinese Church, redeemed by the blood of Christ, not be utterly ruined, and unless you may wish, while recognising the evident danger, that the Emperor be enraged by a renewed provocation. | But do you believe that you can with impunity prod the lion that you think is asleep? We in Beijing, who have known the Emperor almost from his boyhood, do not cease to tremble with fear that evils similar to previous ones will occur, or even greater evils if, scorning the command of His Majesty, the missionaries do not bother to await the pontifical decision, but in accordance with the regulation of the Lord Patriarch publicly endeavour to abolish the Chinese rites.

### 8. He shows the two-fold error in which Father Fernández is involved

I hear that Your Reverend Paternity bases your boldness of spirit in making light of the present danger on these factors: (1) that the Emperor is said to have been appeased by the humble letter of the Lord Patriarch, presenting an apology for the offences; (2) that Your Reverend Paternity, notwithstanding the publication of the regulation in Ji’nan fu by you, has received an imperial permit in Linqing granting permission to remain in China; and (3) that from Palm Sunday until the present no harm has resulted from the said publication.

<!-- PDF page 592 | book page 582 -->

I reply to the first: it is totally false, for no jot or tittle of writing from the Lord Patriarch has appeared, either mediately or immediately, which might express humility or an apology to the Emperor. It is indeed true that the Rev. Fr. Buonaventura, when he came to Beijing, claimed that the Lord Patriarch said to him on parting that when he found a suitable time and place he would indicate to the Emperor how sorry he was for the offence he had given and how much he wished to offer satisfaction for it; but since he did not bring anything written, any signature, seal, or any other testimony either from the Lord Patriarch or his secretary concerning these matters, this form of apology was not offered to the Emperor by the said Reverend Father, nor could it be offered by anyone.

Response to the 2nd: If Your Paternity obtained a permit easily, you can rejoice even after making the publication, but you cannot act rashly. You need to consider carefully in your own mind what would have happened if the Emperor had known what was done in Ji’nan fu: at the very least you may think that you might have been required to depart from the empire, as happened to the Rev. Fr. Frossolone. But if you were permitted to remain in your mission only because the facts were not known, then the publication of the facts alone will suffice for you to be expelled, and, indeed, with greater severity than that with which others have been expelled.

Hence the response to the 3rd is also clear. For misfortune sometimes passes a person by and sometimes comes upon him; so for 20 years it passed by the Most Reverend Charles Maigrot, Lord Bishop of Conon, and finally came upon him in such a way that the whole mission is exposed to ruin on his account.

### 9. He points out the danger threatening religion

In order that Your Reverend Paternity may recognise the danger to souls arising from what you have done, suppose, I ask you, that all the missionaries in China were to do what you do and have done, and that even the Christians in Beijing were burdened with the regulation of the Lord Patriarch. What, I ask, may I suppose the future will be? Can you imagine that the Emperor will bear patiently that his most recent decree, on which the ink is still scarcely dry, be treated with contempt by the Europeans, even though in it all he asks is that they await the decision of the Holy Father concerning the matters referred to him by His Majesty, and that if the decision comes in favour of the Chinese rites they may peacefully preach the Gospel, and if a contrary decision arrives, they may depart from His empire in safety? I say to you in all seriousness that not even the Catholic King,[^p592-4] the lord of Your Paternity, would bear patiently that a <!-- PDF page 593 | book page 583 | manuscript page 689 --> very moderate law of his, of this kind, be rejected through the indiscreet zeal of some Religious. There is, indeed, nothing to persuade Your Paternity that there is no danger in such transgression of the royal decree, unless it be that you have confidence that no one will reveal this to the Emperor. But it is not the action of a prudent man engaged in the apostolic ministry, who is seeking the conversion and salvation of more souls than there are in the whole of Spain, to wish to rely on the chance that if it goes unnoticed | perhaps all will be well, whereas if he does know, all will certainly and irreparably be lost. Your Paternity may say, if you wish, that the Jesuits are bound to make every effort to conceal from the Emperor what you, without being commanded by any Superior, on your own impulse, want to do with regard to the neophytes. Will we be able to conceal this matter, simply because Your Paternity judges that we ought to do so? You may say that we are able to do this; will you therefore say that we want to? If you say that we want to, you must put aside an opinion held by many, namely that we can and do not want to. We do indeed want to, and insofar as we are able, we earnestly want to; however the matter will not long remain secret, since many people know, and many seek to know. The state of the Christian religion is not the same as it was before the coming of the Lord Patriarch, and the examination of the Most Rev. Bishop of Conon. The very wise Emperor now investigates everything thoroughly and the mandarins do not cease to ask themselves whether, since they know that their lord has been offended by the Europeans, the same things may happen again that the Emperor complains have injured him.

### 10. The pontifical decision has not yet been brought to China

Since mention has been made above of the expected arrival of the pontifical decision, perhaps one might consider that it has come already, since the decision of the Lord Patriarch is said to be in agreement with the pontifical decision, and this must be accepted with full confidence. But the Lord Patriarch himself does not seem to have made this judgment of the situation, as can be seen from what I have noted above; nor was this judgement made by the Most Illustrious Bishop of Ascalon, the Apostolic Vicar in Jiangxi, who, together with three Reverend Franciscan Fathers, made a legal appeal in writing against the decision of the Patriarch; nor was this the judgment of thirty other missionaries, who made a similar appeal; nor was it made by our and your Most Illustrious and Reverend Ordinary, Bishop Bernardino della Chiesa, who[^p593-5] wrote to the Lord Patriarch and ourselves to the effect that, even if the original of the pontifical <!-- PDF page 594 | book page 584 | manuscript page 690 --> decision had arrived, it was by no means opportune for it to be promulgated here and now lest oil and fuel be added to the fire and the flame lay waste the whole mission, far and wide. What has been said so far should suffice to suggest that Your Reverend Paternity should not lay this heavy burden on the Christians and incautiously bring down disaster upon the mission at a time when the ships bringing news from Europe can almost be seen from the Chinese shore. I will not add anything further, lest I seem to be causing offence rather than warning in a friendly fashion. However, on one point I must not be silent, namely that I have written this to Your Paternity with the knowledge of my superiors and in accordance with their wishes. I have also kept a copy of this letter so that, if the need should arise, they will be able to show both in China and Europe that we, sincerely and in a religious spirit, have striven to avert the danger to the whole mission and to the person of Your Reverend Paternity and your people, our brothers in Christ and cherished co-workers in the vineyard of Our Lord. I commend myself, and the struggling mission, to the most holy sacrifices of Your Reverend Paternity.

Beijing, 10th July, 1707.

Your Most Reverend Paternity’s Servant in Christ.

That this agrees with the original, I so testify.

Fr. Kilian Stumpf, SJ, Apostolic Notary.[^p594-6]

### 11. Some things to be observed in the letter of Father Stumpf

In this letter three things are to be noted. First, some of the things that Father Fernández did were confirmed by Father Franchi, but his letters came later; indeed some had clearly been intercepted. Second, at this time in Beijing there was as yet no knowledge of the appeal of the Most Illustrious Lord Bishop of Macau, a copy of which we obtained on July 20th. Third, as regards the allegation that three Franciscan Fathers subscribed to the appeal, this we had learned from Rev. Fr. Buonaventura, who had seen the original copy of the appeal held by the Lord Patriarch; but in what sense they subscribed, even today we still do not know, and we hear that this is disputed, just as there has also been dispute about the sense in which other Franciscan Fathers in Shandong added their signatures and received the permit. We will not dwell on these matters. |

Further, as regards the effect of this letter, the reader will learn enough about this from the letters of Father Franchi which are now to be quoted. I will provide an even more extended account of these, because much depends on them; since they have made great use of them against the Beijing Fathers and others, <!-- PDF page 595 | book page 585 --> making public suspicions about them etc. But before I get on to what happened, I should include here the letter written by the same Father on 16 July 1707 to Fr. José Soares.

### 12. Letter of Father Girolamo Franchi to Father José Soares, 16 July 1707

Reverend Father,

Very gradually, from far off, news has arrived here. About eight days ago, one or another of the Christians came to Father da Serravalle (he was a guest of Father Fernández) and asked whether he might venture to confess to me and hear the Mass that I was celebrating. Father da Serravalle turned them away, pretending not to understand. They came with the same question to Father Fernández, either beforehand or afterwards. What he replied I do not know. Three days ago, when I received a letter warning me to act cautiously since the Prince had spies everywhere, I called together the catechists of this residence and reported the matter to them, and told them to be prudent lest they endanger the mission; hence they should not touch upon these matters if there was no need to do so. But if on some occasion the question should arise, for example when they were speaking in the presence of educated non-Christians, you can say I said that these rites are not understood by the Emperor in an atheistic sense, since last year he said that Jing Tian is not about worshipping a material heaven.

Besides the catechists of our Church, with whom I spoke in my room, two other Christians were present. One of these asked me this question: “Now that the Holy Father’s zhiyi[^p595-7] has been promulgated (namely by Reverend Father Fernández) what will we do?” I replied: “If there is no necessity, do not speak of these matters. Moreover, by briefly reporting what the Emperor said you are not going against the decree, nor are you violating the Emperor’s zhiyi. Just as, if you were to hear your friend saying that he wanted to commit some crime and you were to report this to me, you would not be considered to be committing it, so, in the same way, we are acting in the present case.” And I sent them away, urging them to pray fervently to God. For if the Holy Father, having heard the views of the Emperor, declares these rites to be illicit, there seems to be no hope, humanly speaking, of preserving our religion in China; they themselves admit this, since the decisions of the Holy See in matters of faith must be observed by all, especially as the Holy See now has heard from the Emperor himself his views.

<!-- PDF page 596 | book page 586 | manuscript page 691 -->

After lunch I went to Father da Serravalle. Shortly afterwards Father Fernández arrived, and asked in an angry voice and with an angry look what I had said to the Christians. I replied that I had said what I ought to say, namely that they should be cautious. Then he answered that I had made known to them the royal edicts; but since I had not yet made known to them the decrees of the Holy See, I had instructed them contrary to the regulation (of the Lord Patriarch). I replied that not the slightest word had been spoken by me against the regulation and that I had said various things which were in no way contrary to the regulation. Then he declared that the things that are said about spies are scare tactics on the part of the Beijing Fathers, who even in years past—I don’t know what occasion he was talking about—used say the same. Today I called together the catechists, who still remembered what I had said the day before yesterday. They were ready to testify that I said nothing that might encourage them to go against the decrees either of the Holy Father or of the Lord Patriarch, and that I said nothing that was opposed to the decrees which were promulgated in my absence by Father Fernández. I must add that Father Fernández on that same day, when I wished to return home, apologised that he had become angry in this way, adding that he did so on account of Father Serravalle, who was sent by the Sacred Congregation and was under oath to the Sacred | Congregation; hence, he was constrained to make a show in this way lest he also should seem to be in favour of or following the ancient practices, even after the regulation of the Lord Patriarch.[^p596-8] I suspend my judgement as to whether this is true or not; it seems however that even an honourable goal should not be sought by such means, and with such a clamour … Ji’nan fu 16th July 1707.

To Your Reverence. Your most humble and obedient servant,

Girolamo Franchi.

PS. I am sending the order of the Viceroy about the investigation into the Europeans.

That this agrees with the original, I so testify.

Fr. Kilian Stumpf, SJ, Notary Apostolic.

### 13. A second letter of the same Father Girolamo Franchi, to Father Kilian

Reverend in Christ Father Kilian,

I received yesterday the letter of Your Reverence to Reverend Father Fernández. Ich habe das gelesen und abgeschrieben (that is: I have read it and made a copy).[^p597-9] Although the weather was rainy, I went myself yesterday to the aforementioned Reverend Father and, in the presence of Rev. Fr. Serravalle, I handed over your letter. While he was reading it at the invitation of Rev. Fr. Serravalle, I began to play dice. A little after he had read it, reacting angrily as is usual for him on such occasions and in a loud voice—the Christian painter Vitalis was actually painting in his room, the door of which opens immediately onto the little ting[^p597-10] in which we were—he began to protest and question the fact of Your Reverence having been notified by the Christians that he had urged them to follow the regulation of the Lord Patriarch. He however knew that no Christians of this city could have written anything about these matters, and if anything was written by anyone, it was done at my instigation: he would investigate the matter and discover the truth. Remaining calm, as Fr. da Serravalle will bear witness, I replied: “Your Paternity, as you have so often been mistaken in your imprudent judgements, so you are mistaken now. None of the Christians wrote anything about these matters at my instigation or with my knowledge” (I report here only the main things said.) Then the Reverend Father said that I had incurred excommunication. I replied that I had little fear of excommunication, which I had not incurred, nor could it be proved that I had incurred it. When Fr. Serravalle heard him shouting he immediately put aside the dice, got up, went out of the ting, and began to walk in the hall in front of the ting, saying that he wanted to know nothing about these things. Then Father Fernández shouted, “Let Father Giovanni Battista (Serravalle) be witness that this fellow does not fear excommunication.” I quietly declared again, “I have said that I do not fear an excommunication which I have not incurred, and which Your Reverence cannot prove me to have incurred.” Then he replied, “I prove it.” “Well, then, do so,” I said. He wished to prove, first, that I did not hold in my heart what the regulation prescribes; second, that in Tai’an I had not pulled down the [ Jing Tian] tablet; third, that I had not published the regulation; fourth, that I set myself against the regulation, on account of the things I had said to my catechists on the 14th of this <!-- PDF page 598 | book page 588 | manuscript page 692 --> month; fifth, that since the regulation had been published here by himself, and I remained silent, saying nothing to the Christians about the observance of the regulation, I am in contravention of the regulation. I at once made a response to each individual objection, and I annex all of them together here. To the first, that the heart of a human being is known only to God; to the second and third, that the Lord Patriarch did not issue commands to do so; to the fourth, I have witnesses of what I said, nothing of what I said was opposed to the regulation, and I never said the slightest word to any of the Christians expressing disapproval of his publication of the regulation; however I said to my catechists that if the catechists do as I say they are not opposing the regulation. In the same way, if someone were to say, “Arius teaches that the Son is less than the Father,” if he simply reports this, so long as he does not believe it, or show that he believes it, or persuade others to believe it, he is not considered to err against the faith, even though the opinion of Arius is certainly contrary to the faith. As for the fifth, this is not a command of the Lord Patriarch. I then added that | the Lord Bishop of Ascalon had made an appeal. Then the Reverend Father Fernández said: “The Bishop of Ascalon also has incurred excommunication.” I responded: “That remains to be proved.” “I prove it,” he said; but nevertheless he brought forward no proofs. He then added that Your Reverence is naïve, that he follows the example of Reverend Father Frossolone, who was ejected on the initiative of the Fathers or by the Fathers of the Society. He said that he also is prepared to be exiled, and, indeed, to die for this cause; in former times there were other Franciscans who were martyrs in the service of the Church, because of obedience. He stated that he had suffered because he adhered to us in the past and now once again has to suffer because he is obedient to the Lord Patriarch and to his bishop, and that the practices of the Society he now regards as certainly erroneous. However, if the Holy Father had decided otherwise, he would at once have followed those practices. The Fathers of the Society want the other missionaries to be under their feet. Now that the Holy Father has come to a decision against the Society notwithstanding the representations of the Emperor, the Fathers of the Society will find other pretexts to send others to Rome with new reports etc. Father da Serravalle again indicated that he would remain silent, and so once again we amused ourselves with dice. A little later Fr. Fernández returned and gave me to read the aforementioned letter sent to him. It was written by Rev. Fr. Frossolone, and subscribed to by the Most Illustrious Bishop of Beijing. There one could read in Spanish to this effect: “I am greatly surprised at this situation, that the regulation has not yet been published; for this is what the regulation prescribes when it says to teach.” I replied: “Your Paternity knows that I have never complained to you or to the Christians that you published the regulation.” Further (I did not say this to the Franciscan Fathers) it seems <!-- PDF page 599 | book page 589 --> that Father Frossolone, who wrote that letter, was forgetful of his logic, since he reasoned in this way: the regulation prescribes that one teach, therefore it prescribes that one publishes. For one who prescribes that one teach, does not prescribe that one teaches everything … What he will write back to Your Reverence I do not know. It is to be hoped that he will not reply with acrimony, especially if he writes with the awareness of the presence of Rev. Fr. Serravalle, who now three times has heard him speaking angrily to me and has heard my peaceful replies; once when I was absent he reproved him on account of what happened on the 14th of this month, adding that he should not have been angry, especially as he saw that I replied with moderation. He replied that he knew he was in the wrong, and that was why he replied with moderation. You can see what great troubles I have with this Religious. I humbly commend myself to Your Reverence’s most holy sacrifices and prayers. Ji’nan fu, 18 July 1707.

That this agrees with the original, I so testify.[^p599-11]

Fr. Kilian Stumpf, SJ, Notary Apostolic.

### 14. In what spirit Father Fernández received the letter of Father Kilian

As is clear from this letter, Rev. Fr. Fernández did not receive the letter of Father Kilian in a tranquil frame of mind, nor did he perceive that what was intended was the common good of the mission and a peaceful situation for the Reverend Franciscan Fathers in the province nearest to the court. Rather, he imagined the opposite, as Father Franchi wrote on August 18th in these words: “Rev. Fr. Fernández, and Rev. Fr. Serravalle too, do not understand certain words of the letter written by Your Reverence, and although I explained them to them, they still do not grasp the meaning, or do not wish to grasp it; rather, they understand them in a quite false and perverse sense; and this proceeds, in my judgment, from either a lack of knowledge of the Latin language or on account of the calumnies spread about the Beijing [Fathers], because they are capable of doing so yet do not want to. For from the words “will you therefore say that we want to” etc. they infer that the Fathers in Beijing do not want to. Secondly, the words “at a time when the European ships can almost be seen from the Chinese shore” are, they say, a threat that the Reverend Father Fernández is to be sent back to Europe on the ships that are about to return there.” In the end, Rev. Fr. Fernández made no further reply to the said letter of Father Kilian, considering it sufficient to say that it was being sent to the Lord Patriarch to be forwarded on his orders to Europe to be concocted into something by somebody or other.

What I have reported concerning this Reverend Father and his endeavours will suffice for July; nor will I set out more of the things preserved in the records for the last fifteen years concerning what he has written | and what he has done. Under the following August it will be necessary to make mention of him again.

In these days some Christians in Beijing had come to understand that it was being said by the Lord Patriarch that in this capital city the Christians did not have in their homes the tablets of the dead; hence some of them went to the Rev. Fr. Buonaventura da Roma of the Order of St. Francis, freely confessing that they were keeping the tablets in common use. I will report what happened in the words the same Reverend Father wrote in his own hand.

### 15. The Christians of Beijing bear witness to the fact that they have in their homes tablets of the dead

July 16th. I, the undersigned, was sent by the Most Illustrious and Most Reverend Lord Patriarch of Antioch and General Visitor, to Beijing to receive the royal residence permit which would enable me to remain in the Chinese Empire. On the occasion of receiving the said letter, I was obliged to stay for many days in the months of June and July, and a number of Christians came to me of their own accord: Paul Zhai, Lawrence Zhu, Joseph Xian, Matthias Ke, Peter Wang, Philip Sun, Michael Fang, Matthias Bao, and Alexander Bao. All these were learned men, and they said that it had become known in Beijing that the Most Illustrious Lord Patriarch was asserting throughout the provinces that in the previous year the Christians at the royal court had been individually questioned by him as to whether, according to the custom of the Imperial House, they had the tablets of their deceased parents. Each and every one (except for one old man) gave the response that they did not have such tablets. In contradiction to this the said Christians declared that they were coming to me on that day to testify in due form, before God and men, that they had never before heard of such a response of the Christians to the Most Illustrious and Most Reverend Lord Patriarch. Further, they had always had, and still have, these tablets. They were ready to produce them before my eyes and even, if I should wish it, call together a thousand Christians living in this royal capital who would all likewise produce their tablets for me to see. The said Christians also asked that I should gather thoroughly accurate information by which the falsity of the rumour, which they attributed especially to the interpreter of the Lord Patriarch,[^p600-12] might be conclusively demonstrated. I truthfully replied that I did not have the authority to gather such information and consoled <!-- PDF page 601 | book page 591 | manuscript page 694 --> them, advising that they calmly and peacefully await a further decision of the Most Illustrious Lord Patriarch and the Holy See.

Nevertheless, since it was so grave a matter affecting the whole mission, or rather because I had an obligation to satisfy my own conscience which was endangered, I wished to attest in my own hand, on the word of a priest, to what I remembered of the testimony offered by the Christians who have been named and the whole affair as it actually took place. At Beijing in the College of the Society of Jesus, 16 July 1707.

### 16. Father Buonaventura da Roma leaves Beijing, with Father Simões

On July 17th the Reverend Father Buonaventura da Roma departed from Beijing and headed for Nanjing, together with Father Simões, SJ; a fever that had come upon him prevented him from making an earlier departure. Dr. Borghese provided him with powdered Kina Chinae,[^p601-13] which the sick man took from the hand of Father Kilian. Although he at first wished to blend the powder with sugar of roses to make it a palatable medicine, as some people do in Europe—in which case it would have no other effect than to cause vomiting, which would be totally useless for curing the disease—again and again he took it from his hand, blended with wine, and he regained his strength. There was no suspicion of anything sinister although, while on the Patriarch’s boat, he had read and so often heard that the Fathers in Beijing had come under suspicion of presenting poison to the Lord Patriarch. The same Reverend Father in a letter of the 15th, which is preserved here, with great politeness bade farewell to Father Antoine, who was staying in the Eastern Church, thanking him for the kindness that had been shown him. He did the same at the College, where he had been a guest up to this time, offering 10 ounces of silver for his board and lodging. | None of the silver was accepted. The reply was given that although his courtesy was praiseworthy and worth remembering, and would be so noted, nevertheless he should remember, lest he feel offended, that we had never accepted from any religious who was our guest in Beijing payment for board and lodging, or any compensation. Rather, we had given something as provision for the journey <!-- PDF page 602 | book page 592 --> insofar as our slender means allowed. At this time, indeed, our expenses were great on account of the constant visits of our men coming to receive residence permits, and because the building of the Church was not yet complete; so, we begged his forgiveness if we showed ourselves less generous than we would like to be.

### 17. Critics are wrong when they accuse the Jesuits of extravagance in their clothing

Dr. Borghese desired very much to be a companion of the Rev. Fr. Buonaventura in his journey; but since permission could not be obtained from the Emperor, he entrusted to him part of his luggage to be conveyed in advance, until the remainder could be sent after it. The same gentleman asked a similar favour of Father Simões, and from each Father he obtained some part of the request he had made. Among other things, he sent on in advance fur clothing, which he had had made in Beijing at no small expense, as was mentioned in November 1706, and which, as he said, he had prepared as a gift for the Holy Father. This thought, just as it was not particularly praiseworthy, so also involved nothing of censure, and is more honourable than the intention of those who sent Chinese clothing to Paris by the agency of Fr. Jean Pin[^p602-14] and showed them in Rome by the agency of Fr. Charmot[^p602-15] as if they were the clothing of the missionaries, complaining that they were compelled to such extravagance in China by the Fathers of the Society. However, this year the Emperor, the Prince, and the court observed to the courtiers that the reverend clerics clearly had more money than the Jesuits, since they far outclassed them in the way they dressed.

This month some more people warned how great a storm was threatening against the Fathers in Beijing from the Lord Patriarch; more people also encouraged them to compose an appeal. This advice was renewed insistently in the months of August, September, and October by others also who, wishing to support this project, commissioned Father Kilian to carry it out in their name. This is shown in various letters, some of which are preserved in the archives while others were sent to Rome. However the Fathers in Beijing were of the opinion that nothing should be done precipitately, especially since they were still awaiting the response of the Lord Patriarch concerning their joint representation, which they had humbly made to him; they had certain knowledge that <!-- PDF page 603 | book page 593 | manuscript page 695 --> this had been handed to him, both from the letter of Father Vice-Provincial José Monteiro, previously referred to in May, and from the testimony of Father Buonaventura, who was then present on the boat.

In June, above, I made mention of a certain pamphlet printed by Christians in Hangzhou. I will now deal with it more fully, and although its history is drawn out over many months from various occurrences, nevertheless I think it is opportune to tell here the whole story in one continuous narrative lest the details of the affair, small in themselves, when spread out over months become lost in the mind of the reader rather than remain in the reader’s memory. And I wish them to remain there, so that the reader may perceive the notable calumny brought against the missionaries of the Society, both individually and in common. Without doubt this was fully transmitted to Rome in writing by the Patriarch’s supporters before the truth of the matter was ascertained in China. This was what happened.

### 18. The Christian cause is gravely damaged

As soon as the decree of the Emperor against the Fathers Hervé, San Giorgio, etc. had been promulgated in Nanjing, the gentiles began to take up arms against the flock of Christ and its pastors or suffragan pastors. Their boldness increased when they saw that many had already been banished from the Emperor’s presence in Hangzhou. Although His Majesty, in his active benevolence, had taken care | that, while he had their given orders for more than ten missionaries to leave China, he might not seem at the same time to be favouring the enemies of our Holy Law, nevertheless, when, in the case of the Rev. Fr. Cróquer, there began to appear throughout China orders both of mandarins and their lackeys, seeking out the missionaries in order to seize them, the ungodly populace took it that they had permission to vomit out the dark hatred that for a long time they had held in their hearts, and they began to do so. It was not only the general populace who did this, but also, and above all, the scholars, who sharpened their pens and took up bricks and stones against the followers of Christ and their churches. The Christians trembled with fear at such dark clouds approaching. Some fled, turning to the worship of idols; some laid low, concealing their practice of the holy faith; others, with greater daring, decided to defend both themselves and the Holy Law, not with arms but with arguments and writings by which they might, as far as possible, maintain their innocence against the calumny of the learned class. There were also certain friendly gentiles who wished to use their authority to take care of both the Europeans and the Christians who were their compatriots. Among these in Nanjing there was one who, out of kindness, sent certain letters to be attached to our Church, proclaiming to anyone approaching it that the exile of others should not be in any way detrimental to those <!-- PDF page 604 | book page 594 --> whom the Emperor had protected by an official document. In September there will be further consideration of this matter.

### 19. Especially in the city of Hangzhou

But already in Hangzhou a more terrible storm was building. This was where in about the year 1691, when Father Intorcetta was alive, the Governor of that time Zhang Pengge[^p604-16] had stirred up a savage persecution and the Church itself had then been burnt down by hostile people. Now Zhang Pengge and his assistants who were still alive took advantage of the situation, to satisfy their hatred. In order to provide some response to their attacks, a Christian scholar named Ignatius Zhang Xingyao,[^p604-17] a graduate and a man of great courage, composed a pamphlet in which he attempted to make clear to all that the gentiles who blaspheme against our most holy religion are ignorant of its true nature. He put together this composition of his with the help of other Christians, but not with the help of our Fathers, and he made three very notable errors which were in fact harmful to the Christian mission rather than of assistance to it.

### 20. A cause of the persecution: a pamphlet by a Christian scholar written without sufficient prudence

For, firstly, he endeavoured, after the regulation of the Lord Patriarch, to demonstrate that the one whom the ancient and modern Chinese, who are not atheists, worship under the name of Shangdi is the true, living God whom Christians adore. But he did this because the precepts contained in the regulation of the Patriarch had not yet been promulgated to him. Secondly, because he knew that the main enemies of the Holy Law now wished to base their arguments on the authority of Zhang Pengge and <!-- PDF page 605 | book page 595 | manuscript page 696 --> what he had done seventeen years before in that city and province. This being the case, he tried to prove that what Zhang Pengge had said proceeded from ignorance. By doing so he offended a man who was an enemy of old and was now far more celebrated for doctrine, power, and the dignity of his office, since he held a post higher than that of all the Viceroys and Governors; this should have led him to fear greater evils.[^p605-18] Thirdly, he made an attack upon idolatrous sects, rightly wishing to praise the Christian Law, and unwisely he condemned the confusion of idolaters; but this was not the right time to do so, as it was likely to stir up even more the uneducated mob which was madly committed to idols. When the book had been completed he offered it to Fr. Agostino Barelli, then to Fr. Contancin, so that it might come out under the name of the latter or the former. The former could not adopt it as his own, since it was known that he did not yet know how to write Chinese, nor did he yet speak Chinese fluently enough. The latter refused, since he shuddered at the mistakes referred to. In the meantime, however, the pamphlet was printed in the general name of the Christians in Hangzhou, and there were not wanting some who gave it some approbation | in private.[^p605-19] At this the Christians became bolder and asked Father Agostino Barelli, the missionary there, to offer it to the mandarins, knowing that if it were favourably received it could fruitfully appear in the public arena. To this end they explained the matter to the Father, only so far as they judged it necessary to persuade him, and were silent about what they thought might be problematic.

<!-- PDF page 606 | book page 596 -->

### 21. Father Barelli incautiously at first distributed the pamphlet; but he soon suppressed it

So, in good faith, Father Barelli distributed the pamphlet to the officials of that city and sent it elsewhere to our Fathers, and in particular to Songjiang. Other Fathers, who were already skilled in Chinese, were astounded at the audacity of the Christians and warned how much danger could arise from this action rather than help. When Father Barelli realised the error of his Christians, disregarding the trouble to himself, he without delay collected from the houses of the mandarins the copies that had been distributed, hid away the woodblocks, and forbade that they be used again. He sent one copy to Beijing, informing his Superiors what had happened through his imprudence and seeking to be directed by them as to what might seem best in the Lord.

When the Fathers in Beijing had read the letter, Father Kilian was commissioned to reply to the effect that, by every possible means, and with care, he should remove the said pamphlet even from the hands of the Christians, so that he neither give greater provocation to the gentiles nor provide the Lord Patriarch with an opportunity to complain about us. But since the whole affair took place without formal guilt on the part of the Father, there seemed, here and now, to be no need for anything other than that prudence and caution be observed in the future.

However it happened that on the 12th of the month of July the Rev. Fr. Frossolone, travelling to Guangzhou from Linqing and in the process of departing from China in accordance with the command of the Emperor, arrived in Hangzhou, where he was supposed to immediately board the assigned boat, but first he was diverted to the church. Already the edicts of the mandarins concerning the supposed flight of the Rev. Fr. Cróquer etc. had been made public, and the customs officer was unwilling to allow this unknown European to sail; therefore they led him to the church, even though he was unwilling to go, so that recognised by Father Barelli he might confirm his testimony concerning himself. Father Barelli, having sent a note of greeting to the customs officers, requested permission for him to sail and Father Frossolone continued his journey as far as Quzhou,[^p606-20] accompanied by a certain Christian called Thomas Yu. This same Christian had urgently requested from Father Barelli an introduction to the mandarin of the place, to whom, unbeknown to Father Barelli, he brought a copy of the pamphlet.

### 22. A copy of it comes into the hands of Father Frossolone the Franciscan, and it comes to be known in Guangzhou

While on his journey the Rev. Fr. Frossolone come to know about the introduction and the book; he saw the book, disapproved of it, blotted out the characters for Shangdi with black ink wherever they occurred, and said that the book should not be presented to the mandarin but rather should remain in the hands of the Christian. He himself took away the visiting card and the testimonials which had been entrusted by Fr. Barelli to the Christian because the latter had intended to present them to the mandarin together with the book. However, others write more plausibly that Fr. Frossolone took away with him both the book and the introduction. The diversity of these accounts is due to the Christian Thomas Yu, who gave different versions of the event, as will be clear a little later from the letter of Fr. Laureati. Some original letters of Fathers Barelli and Kilian, and also of Fr. Contancin, concerning this affair, were sent in the same year of 1707 to Rome with our procurators. Many months after the said deed, the Reverend Fr. Frossolone made the affair public when he arrived in Guangzhou in September. He wrote from there to the Most Illustrious Bishop of Beijing, who wrote to us. We then wrote to Fr. Barelli, although rather obscurely, since the prime author of the deed had taken considerable care that the true facts of the matter not come soon to the ears of the Jesuits.

### 23. Much righteous anger is kindled against the Jesuits over this

This matter was already seen far differently in Guangzhou, where it was being said that the Jesuits, after the decree of the Lord Patriarch, were insolently defending in printed books that the word Shangdi means the true God etc. But how these matters came to be set forth in writing in the city will become clear at the appropriate time. This at least is certain, that on behalf of the Lord Patriarch a Chinese man was sent from Guangzhou to Ningbo so that thence | he might receive from the English ship letters for the Lord Patriarch brought from Europe. He was given explicit orders that on his return journey to Guangzhou he should turn aside to Hangzhou and there seek out one or two copies of the pamphlet—which, however, he did not do, because Fr. Gollet[^p607-21] who had heard about it intervened and told him that neither the books nor the printing blocks were any longer available.

However, since those who had sought the book in Guangzhou were not thereby satisfied, they tried other means. Whether they got hold of a copy or <!-- PDF page 608 | book page 598 --> not, we do not know. However, we know that the Most Illustrious Bishop of Beijing warned the Fathers in Beijing through Fr. Franchi that the Society would suffer and that the book would be ascribed to one or all of us. This was in the month of December 1707.

### 24. The Jesuits were not the authors of the pamphlet. This is proved firstly from the testimony of the true author

Fr. Barelli, having been advised of the problem both by the Fathers at Beijing and those at Guangzhou, and later by Fr. Gollet, sent an oath at once after the departure of the ships justifying himself to the procurator of the Vice-Province, since there was no other way of justifying himself without serious injury to the Christian mission. Afterwards however he received from the author himself a copy written in his own hand—thereby expressly acknowledging it as his own—and endorsed by two of the Christians, along with the testimony of the Christians.[^p608-22] And when the procurator also in this same year had understood that in Linqing efforts were still being made by the people in Guangzhou to seize upon this fictitious fault of the Jesuits, the Father Procurator, following the wishes of the Vice- Provincial and the Rector at Guangzhou, sent a statement and a letter to Macau. In the statement he made a public protest about the calumny fastened upon us, whether in common or on particular persons, and he made known that we would take action in Rome before the appropriate judge against the calumniators; in the letter he enjoined the Rev. Fr. Frossolone either to retract or to be prepared to respond in Rome concerning this false accusation.

Now I will demonstrate what I have said so far by means of the letters appended here, which have been accurately transcribed, leaving to the reader the freedom to make appropriate application of each one.

### 30. An excerpt from a letter of Father Franchi

I must not omit a paragraph from the letter of Father Girolamo Franchi, which has come into my hands this very moment and which proves again that what has been said so far about what the Rev. Father Frossolone did is indeed true. Rev. Father Frossolone, out of zeal, as I think, both for the glory of God and for the Holy See, while passing through one of our churches, found there, when the missionary was absent, a book defending Shangdi, together with a tiezi of a certain one of our Fathers (the name has been suppressed). The tiezi indicated that the book was directed by that priest of ours to a mandarin, or to another Chinese person in a certain dimian.[^p614-38] The Rev. Fr. Frossolone intercepted the aforementioned book. He himself admitted it when he wrote to the Rev. Fr. da Castorano in Linqing, according to the Most Illustrious Bishop. The above is what Fr. Franchi wrote to Fr. Kilian on 28 March 1708.

That this agrees with the original, I so testify.

Fr. Kilian Stumpf, SJ, Notary Apostolic.

In the text of the letter as given here there appears to be an error in the circumstances of the case, but what it says of the action of Rev. Fr. Frossolone, which is what is dealt with here, is true.

### 31. The book of the scholar was not written against the decision of the Lord Patriarch or in favour of observing the declaration of the Emperor

Fr. Carlo da Castorano had asked Father Kilian in December to provide himself and the Lord Patriarch with the name of the author of the book. The Father gave it but he was troubled by a scruple, fearing that someone, having obtained the information from the Lord Bishop’s procurator, should wish to make ill use of it. He cut out the Chinese name from the letter to Father Castorano, who had written on the matter on 21 January 1708. This was a letter to the Most Illustrious Lord (the Bishop of Beijing) written after the decree of the Most Excellent Lord Patriarch, declaring that the Chinese book was contrary to that decision and in favour of the declaration of the Emperor. For this reason the Most Illustrious Lord wished to know the author of the book, since he was more or less implying <!-- PDF page 615 | book page 605 | manuscript page 701 --> that we Fathers either were the authors or at least knew who they were. He could not read the name (of the author), which he said was illegible in his letter; perhaps it had been crossed out. So Father Castorano, although he spoke more favourably concerning the calumny aimed at the Jesuits by Father Frossolone, did not weaken what the Most Illustrious Lord Bishop said to Father Franchi about the matter. But Father Frossolone also, regarding the present allegation, has directed a calumny against the book itself, maintaining that it was printed against the decision of the Patriarch and in favour of the declaration of the Emperor. This is false, for the book makes no mention of the said decision or the said declaration, | nor does it make any refutation of the matters contained in the decision.

### 32. What is dealt with in the book and for what purpose it was written

It simply and solely deals with this matter; namely, to show the gentiles that our holy religion is in no way deserving of their hatred. In the process of doing so it uses the word Shangdi, wishing to show that the ancient Chinese understood by this word the true God. Now the Holy Father, and by his authority the Lord Patriarch, can prohibit the use of this word in the future, but neither the Holy Father nor the Lord Patriarch has defined, nor ever will define, that the Chinese have not, for three thousand years, acknowledged the true God by means of that word.

I conclude firstly that it is certain that the said book was written in Hangzhou under the aforementioned title, and was printed. The book itself, existing today in our archives in Beijing, testifies to this.

Secondly, it is certain that the book was composed by a Christian scholar with two others helping him; this is proved by the fact that they freely acknowledge, in their own hand, that they are the authors of the book.

Thirdly, it was composed and published in the name of all the Christians in Hangzhou; this is demonstrated conclusively by the letter and by the account of the matter given by the Christians, in which they freely acknowledge the full succession of events.

Fourthly, it is certain that it was not composed by Father Barelli, since apart from the reasons already given, it is certain that the Father cannot yet speak Chinese sufficiently well, much less compose in Chinese.

Fifthly, it is certain that our Fathers in Beijing knew nothing of it, nor did they cooperate in any way. This is shown by the time factor, for the book was printed in Hangzhou at the time when the Jesuits in Beijing, involved in many matters, were not able so suddenly to be free to travel to and fro; this would have been necessary in order to complete the cutting of the printing blocks and the printing of the book in Hangzhou. Moreover the text of the book clearly shows that <!-- PDF page 616 | book page 606 | manuscript page 702 --> it was made and designed for that very place, Hangzhou, where there had been a renewal of the persecution set in motion about seventeen years earlier by Zhang Pengge, who was still alive. Finally, the letter of Father Barelli, in which he laments his fate to Father Grimaldi and asks pardon for his error in distributing some copies, clearly frees the Fathers in Beijing of any suspicion of cooperation.

Sixthly, it is certain that other Fathers of the Society in Songjiang, Suzhou, Shanghai, and other nearby places did not cooperate. This is clear from the reprimands they sent to Father Barelli and also from the confession of the Father in his letter to Father Grimaldi, in which he says that he came to know the error from these reprimands.

Seventhly, it is certain that the Father, through a genuine error, distributed copies to 4 or 5 mandarins in Hangzhou, but once he had come to know his error he corrected it at once by collecting the copies that had been distributed, prohibiting the Christians from printing any more and destroying the printing blocks themselves.

Eighthly, it is also certain that the Father cleared himself by an oath. Therefore it is certain that those are guilty of calumny against him and the rest of the Jesuits who attribute to him or to them the said book, its printing, its publication, etc. It remains true that the author of the book is called Ignatius, and hence perhaps they wish to fasten the blame on the followers of Ignatius.

I, Father Kilian Stumpf, SJ, Procurator and Notary Apostolic, testify that nothing is missing.
