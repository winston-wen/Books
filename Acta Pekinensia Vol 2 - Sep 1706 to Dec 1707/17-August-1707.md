### 1. Order of events to be narrated under this month

While August of last year was very busy, August of the present year in Beijing was rather lacking in incident. The only activity of the Fathers I can point to was that they asked His Majesty that he should show indulgence to the Most Illustrious Lord Bishop of Ascalon, an old man, ill, and impeded in many ways, that he should not be expelled by the mandarins from China before he could come to the court to seek the permit. At first sight, this may seem an easy matter but it involved a great deal of conversation and written communication. The Most Illustrious Bishop of Ascalon bitterly complained that this act of service did not serve his interests.[^p617-1] But in this matter Fr. Kilian made many excuses for himself and others. This whole matter could be passed over in silence if it could have taken place solely between His Most Illustrious Lordship and Fr. Kilian, because the former in accordance with his singular prudence could have easily overlooked a very involuntary error in the interpretation of his wishes by the Father. But we had not known how fearful he was lest anywhere a trap should be prepared by one who had not accepted the appeal.[^p617-2] Hence I think it necessary that I should bring up more accurately a few matters regarding the case, so that its conduct and end may be rightly understood.

In this month also different matters were reported from Guangzhou bearing on the case mentioned. There came many complaints from the provinces concerning harassment on the part of the pagans and the mandarins, and a very harmful rumour was voiced abroad that the Franciscan Fathers in Shandong had declared even in letters they wrote that they had received the permit without promising that they would not in the meantime preach against the Chinese Rites. Secondly, they had openly declared that they would observe the regulation of His Lordship the Patriarch. Thirdly, they claimed that Fathers Bouvet and Jartoux had quite wrongly reported their words to the Prince and consequently to His Majesty. Finally, towards the end of August, news spread abroad of a new catechism printed by the Most Rev. Fr. Mezzafalce containing some novelties. He had published this in 1706, before the Most Reverend Father had been called to Beijing.

These matters are best mentioned at the beginning of the month and briefly summed up in a synopsis. In fact, though, because they are separated both in time and place it is difficult to understand them and relate them properly. However, I will relate them, so far as I can, in the hope that the reader will indulge me patiently.

### 2. Letter of Fr. José Monteiro to Fr. Antoine Thomas

On August 1st., Rev. Fr. José Monteiro, still Vice-Provincial, wrote to Beijing to Fr. Antoine Thomas, Vice-Rector, the following:

I have sent to Macau an appeal that the Fathers in Yangzhou (he should have said in Sanchahe, a district near the city of Yangzhou) made. Since His Lordship the Bishop of Macau does not recognize the jurisdiction of the Lord Patriarch I have doubts that he will try to present it to him, or if he does, certainly it will not be accepted, just as he has already done in the cases of the Bishops of Ascalon and of Macau. And this will be the case if the Bishop of Beijing or all your reverences should wish to lodge an appeal. In this matter I could ask Father Visitor[^p618-3] to appeal in the name of the whole Society. But His Reverence can decide whether or not to do so. He is so fearful of what the Patriarch will do to him that he has retreated to Foshan.[^p618-4]

That this agrees with the original, I so testify.[^p618-5]

Fr. Kilian Stumpf, SJ, Notary Apostolic.

### 4. Why the Beijing Fathers did not appeal

Note 3. Those at Guangzhou also advised the Fathers at Beijing to appeal, but they did not do so for the reasons already mentioned. None urged them to swear to the Patriarch’s regulation, both because they were not included in the scope of the double decree issued by the Lord Patriarch in the month of January and also because they knew that their Superior, the Rev. Fr. Visitor, was living close to the Lord Patriarch and could appeal, indeed ought to do so, if it seemed to him right in the Lord. He did not appeal in this place where the authority of the Lord Patriarch was disputed.[^p619-7] Indeed when he was asked by the Beijing Fathers, he arranged to send a procurator to the feet of the Apostles and took the opportunity for himself to accompany him. Accordingly he actually went to Rome to follow up the case.

### 5. There follows in the letter of Fr. Monteiro matters concerning the Bishop of Ascalon

In the second part of same letter Fr. Monteiro wrote:

The Bishop of Ascalon has come here to recover from an injury to one of his hands. He says he has given up the idea of an appeal, that he will not accept the piao, and that he will go to Rome this year. Moreover most of the Religious say they will let themselves be thrown out of China before they will accept the piao.

That this agrees with the original, I so testify.

Fr. Kilian Stumpf, SJ, Notary Apostolic.

There is no need to note anything else here except that the rumour was false that the Most Illustrious Bishop of Ascalon had given up his appeal, for the rumour was self-contradictory: on the one hand asserting that the said Most Illustrious Lord had given up the appeal and on the other that he was about to go to Rome to make an appeal personally. It must be conceded that in Guangzhou there was an abundance of contradictory information, and more occurred in the following months when those whose minds had been firmly made up against us unloaded their feelings through injurious calumnies against those in Beijing.

It is as follows: “The Lord Bishop of Ascalon Fray Alvaro is unable to write and says that if the missionaries who are in Guangzhou are exiled, or even if they are not but stay while remaining free to leave China, there is no need to do anything. This narration will outline the steps towards the petition on behalf of the Most Illustrious Bishop of Ascalon made by the Beijing Fathers. It is to be noted first <!-- PDF page 621 | book page 611 --> that the Most Illustrious Bishop of Ascalon in the month of June had his right hand inflamed with an infection, and was unable to complete his next letter from Nan’an to Beijing sent on the 26th of the said month, and in the month of August at Guangzhou for the same reason was unable to write, as is clear from the testimonies of Fathers Monteiro Vice-Provincial and Raymundo Visitor.

Secondly, what he wrote at Guangzhou halfway through the month of August could not be known at the Court until after September. If the Beijing Fathers had postponed petitioning the Emperor until this news had been received at the end of October, the reply could not arrive at Guangzhou until well into December, which would be after the departure of the ships for Europe or when they were in the very act of setting sail, in which case His Most Illustrious Lordship would inevitably have had to depart or to remain, as the Emperor had determined, without leaving him time or place for a reply.

### 7. The Beijing Fathers acted prudently when they sought the permit for the Lord Bishop of Ascalon

Thirdly, it was necessary for the Beijing Fathers to act in accordance with what they gathered was being sought by the Most Illustrious Bishop of Ascalon from his letters in the months of April, May, and June up to the infirmity of his right hand. He, in his prudence taking a long view, advised in the end that nothing should be sought for him in case His Lordship the Patriarch did not defer to his appeal. But it was not clear in Beijing whether the appeal had been made or not. Indeed from the letter of Fr. Monteiro referred to above doubt remained whether after the first of August the Most Illustrious Bishop of Ascalon had decided not to make the approach. For although the reply of His Lordship the Patriarch to the appeal had been signed at Guangzhou on May 29th, it had to be sent to Nan’an, from Nan’an return to Guangzhou, and from Guangzhou be forwarded to Beijing. Even leaving out the occurrence of extraordinary obstacles, this could take an interval of three or four months.

Fourthly, in these circumstances, and considering principally the anxiety for ships to return quickly, it was decided in Beijing in accordance with the letters of the Most Illustrious Bishop written in previous months (because these were all that we had to guide us) to seek in obedience to the said Lordship what seemed most suitable for him, and not unacceptable to the Emperor. Therefore, with the knowledge of superiors, Fr. Kilian composed the following document:

The European Bai Wanle (Chinese name of the Most Illustrious Bishop of Ascalon), from the Kingdom of Spain, of the Order of St. Augustine, aged 66. He came to China 20 years ago. He lives in Nan’an, Jiangxi Province. He followed <!-- PDF page 622 | book page 612 | manuscript page 706 --> the practices of Li Madou. He desires to remain in China. When he heard that His Majesty was coming to Nanbian,[^p622-9] he left for there in order to seek the permit, but was forced by age and infirmity to return home. Therefore on bended knees and with his forehead touching the ground he humbly asks for himself an appointed time within which to come into the presence of His Majesty, or to be granted permission to go to Guangzhou and remain there under the same conditions as the five Fathers of the Society of Jesus and one of the Order of St. Dominic. This request for the time being lacks the signature of His Most Illustrious Lordship.

However I testify that it agrees with the original.

Regarding his petition Fr. Kilian wrote earlier this very month to the Most Illustrious Bishop of Ascalon and requested, since he had committed this matter to Fr. Antoine Thomas etc., that he should approve it as his own lest | there should be any difficulty.

For the time being some matters must be noted <!-- PDF page 624 | book page 614 | manuscript page 707 --> regarding the petition, so that after the unfortunate consequences so unjustly suffered in China a more just opinion may prevail in Europe.

### 10. Why a similar favour was not granted to other missionaries

I. Some indeed have wondered why the Beijing Fathers did not make a similar petition on behalf of other missionaries. The answer is that some did not ask for Jesuit help; others did ask, but for none of them were there the particular reasons which supported His Lordship’s application. I will pass over his old age, bad health, long residence, merits to the mission, and defence of the Emperor’s declaration, matters in which some others have something in common. He surpassed all others with the Emperor in that he had appealed against the regulation of the Lord Patriarch, since | the Emperor had appealed against the same Lord to the Supreme Pontiff, while others then already in the province of Guangdong had accepted the Patriarch’s regulation against the Chinese Rites, had sworn to follow it, and had promulgated it.

### 11. Some observations on the document written on behalf of the Bishop of Ascalon

2. The formula of the petition has seemed to some too humble, but it has no savour of contempt for the person of the Most Illustrious Lord. It is accommodated to the arrogant style of the pagan court of Beijing, to which missionaries out of love of the true God must bow. By a similar formula, the Most Illustrious Bishop of Beijing sought the diploma; his document is tucked away in its place. Still more humble, in the presence of His Tartaro-Chinese Majesty, was the petition of the Most Illustrious Bishop of Conon, His Lordship Charles Maigrot, as can be seen under July and August and in the Acts of the Palace for 1706. For it is the custom here that nobody in the presence of a superior calls himself by an honorific title, and so nobody in the presence of the Emperor uses any other name than his humble and familiar name. Hence, even His Lordship the Patriarch in writing to or speaking with the Emperor was compelled to use only Duoluo, which was the domestic name assumed by His Excellency in China.

3. It was written, it was said, that the Most Illustrious Lord was of the Order of St. Augustine, although he was of episcopal rank. He himself was accustomed to write and rejoice with glory in the memory that he had been raised from the Order of St. Augustine the Bishop to the episcopate, like others who from religious orders have been raised to the purple. Furthermore it was necessary to describe him as from some Order, because Fr. Guéty had most imprudently brought about this necessity in the year 1706. Nor was it allowable to describe him as belonging to the Order of St. Peter because that name had <!-- PDF page 625 | book page 615 | manuscript page 708 --> been besmirched in the case of Fr. Guéty, the Most Illustrious Bishop of Conon, San Giorgio, Hervé, and others through the crass follies perpetrated by the same Fr. Guéty in the same place and year.

4. There is nowhere in the petition a request for permission to leave China, because it was not clear at the time whether the Most Illustrious Lord had made up his mind about this, nor did he ever make clear whether he wanted this conditionally—in this case yes, in the other no. But in the presence of the Emperor it was not allowed to bring forward such variable petitions, so we explicitly asked that he might go to Macau and be able to return from there without any time limitation. As far as the Emperor was concerned all could depart to wherever they wished without receiving the permit.

5. There was no absolute promise of his coming to Beijing to ask for the permit but his reverence for His Chinese Majesty was displaying in his setting out to seek the permit, and seeking dispensations but of an indeterminate kind. Some infer that the mandarins could have been able on the strength of the petition to oblige him to travel to the Court but this does not hold because the Most Illustrious Lord, relying on the same document, was able to recur to the latter as grounds for continuing to delay.

### 12. The Bishop of Ascalon for 20 years followed the practice of Father Matteo Ricci

6. It is the plain truth that the Most Illustrious Lord followed the practice of Father Matteo Ricci. It cannot be denied that he did this in China for twenty years. It is clear also in Europe from documents in his own hand brought to the Pontifical Tribunal, approved and printed. The difficulty concerned the future. But what he wanted to follow in the future could be legitimately presumed from his appeal in the matter to the Holy See against the regulation of the Lord Patriarch. In no way by these words I followed was there a promise that even against the Church of God and a decree of the Supreme Pontiff made after due process, which perhaps was already altered after further more careful inquiry, he would still follow these practices. But neither was this what the Emperor demanded. Nor could it enter the mind of any sensible person to suspect, in the case of one whose good reputation and missionary faith were clear to all, that these words were not freely and spontaneously subjected | to the decision of Holy Mother Church, even with danger of exile and to life as soon as it should be published in due canonical form. The disputations up until now concerned whether the Chinese Rites were evil in themselves or of themselves and, granted that they were not evil, whether it was right to forbid them. But nobody disputed whether the Supreme Pontiff could forbid them even if they were doubtful or even openly licit, or whether a prohibition of this kind, if it were done canonically, had to be observed. For if a good egg prohibited <!-- PDF page 626 | book page 616 --> by Holy Mother (Church) under pain of mortal sin is to be excluded from one’s food, how much more should obedient sons out of reverence for their Holy Mother abstain from it, even though it may not be totally bad, and treat it like a kind of scorpion.[^p626-15]

### 13. It could not be presumed that the appeal of Bishop Ascalon would be rejected

7. The Most Illustrious Lord is alleged in letters written in the month of May to have said that if his appeal was not accepted nothing should be requested except that he should be allowed to remain at Guangzhou, as it were in exile, in imitation of the five Fathers of the Society of Jesus and one of the Order of St. Dominic. However at that time there was no agreement amongst themselves much less among the Beijing Fathers regarding not appealing, as was shown at greater length above. Nonetheless, this very thing was expressly asked for in the petition and precisely on that account we had to avoid in every possible way his being unwillingly expelled by the mandarins, since he had been so helpful to the mission in this calamitous clash of men and events and might prove the sole support of an infant Church already facing ruin. This did not mean that we presumed his appeal would not be passed on. Who would presume this since there was a statement from the Council of Trent that if it is a question of the head of one man, a valid and effective appeal should be made because if an erroneous sentence was passed and put into execution, neither the head to the body nor life to the corpse could be restored when the error was detected. And here it was a question of the exile of missionaries, of danger to life, of the guilt and punishment for treason of new Christians, of the perpetual exclusion of the Holy Gospel from this empire, and the damnation of countless souls. This could neither by His Lordship the Patriarch nor by any other European authority either ecclesiastical or civil be made whole again, and already irreparable harm had followed to some extent and experience had shown that it could not be remedied. Therefore …

But I would weary the reader without good cause if I were to wish to prove that the Lord Patriarch could and ought to have passed on the appeal when he actually did not do so, and one must deal with the effects of this refusal.

What I have said here is sufficient regarding the things which happened in the month of August in the case of the Most Illustrious Lord Bishop of Ascalon. Now a few words about the missionaries in the province of Shandong.

<!-- PDF page 627 | book page 617 | manuscript page 709 -->

In the prologue of this month, I said that a rumour had spread, because we could not track down anything certain and authentic. I called it very harmful because it produced very harmful effects. This is how things were. The Emperor having gone to the South gave many missionaries opportunity during his journey to seek the permit. The first of all of them were Spanish Franciscans in Linqing on the 9th of March, when they had heard not a word about the regulation of His Lordship the Patriarch which had been published on the 7th of February. And so following the example of the Most Illustrious Bishop of Beijing and his assistant Fr. da Castorano, all, not even Fr. Frossolone excepted, in the same way requested the royal permit, and received the promise of it as was narrated under the month of March.[^p627-16]

### 14. Why eight Dominican Fathers with three clerics were expelled from China

Then the Emperor, having learned of the publication of the regulation when he was near Yangzhou, ordered five Fathers of the Society of Jesus to Guangzhou and at Nanjing proscribed the Reverend Fathers San Giorgio and Hervé. In Hangzhou, somewhat unwillingly, he ordered that eight Dominican Fathers should depart from China[^p627-17] and with them three clerics because although they wished to receive the permit, they did not want to make a promise | that they would not preach against the Chinese rites while awaiting the arrival in China of the reply the Supreme Pontiff should give to the information sent by the Emperor. On hearing this, at this point, some of the Society received the royal permit.

### 15. How the Franciscans asked themselves why they accepted the and declared that they accepted it without any condition

Hence there was a great expectation on the part of the Reverend Clerics and the Reverend Dominican Fathers regarding what would happen in the future to the Revpiao erend Franciscan Fathers in Linqing. Also because they knew that now there were among them three Italians sent by the Sacred Congregation for the Propagation of the Faith. And also because it was clear that most of them had published the Patriarch’s regulation at the very time when they were expecting the promised permit of the Emperor. But what happened was that they received the permit from the Emperor on his way back to Beijing, as narrated in June.

<!-- PDF page 628 | book page 618 -->

When this was agreed to be the case some in Guangzhou and elsewhere[^p628-18] argued that the Reverend Fathers had deceitfully taken the oath to the Patriarchal Regulation put before them, because they had had no doubt that the permit would not have been given by the Emperor except with the aforementioned condition; others felt that His Majesty had indulgently remitted the condition. To both the Franciscan Fathers owed a rejoinder. So first they made the joint response transcribed under June. But individually each then wrote something to remove all odium from himself and his companions and to arouse a feeling of compassion, as if they had been persuaded that it was necessary by some outsider’s cajoling.

### 16. Hence various rumours were started

One of them, as seen in June, excused himself on grounds of confusion of mind, some more courageous declared that they had promised nothing contrary to the regulation which they protested that they had openly observed, and their words were unkindly reported to the Prince, and those of the Prince in turn were related to the Franciscan Fathers by two Jesuits. Hence, to pass over the views of others, His Lordship the Patriarch began to take a firm stance against the Beijing Fathers and wished his regulation to be firm and inviolable, saying that it was now clear there was no further danger in publicising and observing the regulation since the Franciscan Fathers had protested in the presence of His Majesty that they were now preaching in accordance with it and yet had been generously given the permit. And so those could conclude who hastened to ascribe the ruin of the mission not to their untimely advice but to others. This rumour quickly filled China because the Reverend Fr. Frossolone, expelled after the permit was denied him, carried it with him during his whole journey from Linqing to Guangzhou, taking care to plant it and make it grow. We will speak of this again in September.

Further, in this matter, Fathers Bouvet and Jartoux were especially in difficulties. They were forced by order of their superiors to defend their reputation. The defence was not easy because there was no definite aggressor, since the Spanish relation produced in June was not yet known in Beijing.[^p628-19] Fighting against such general accusations was like beating the empty air.

### 17. The remedy proposed

The only course of action seemed to be that, in order to oppose rumour to truth, we should write to the Most Illustrious Bishop of Beijing, to inform him that such things were being so rashly spread <!-- PDF page 629 | book page 619 | manuscript page 710 --> abroad. Great harm could come to the Franciscan Fathers if what they dared write to Europeans should come equally to the ears of the Emperor or of the Prince. Moreover because Fr. Jartoux was particularly charged with having falsely reported what was said, he added his oath of purgation. This in the best form he could he offered to the Most Illustrious Lord Bishop his Ordinary, praying with the support of Father Bouvet that the Most Illustrious Lord would prevent innocence being harmed by calumnies and the mission through false statements being brought into more serious danger. |

The authentic form of the oath and of the letters written by the Fathers were translated because they pertained to the French procuratorship, and were sent to Rome through the same, together with the original reply of the Most Illustrious Lord Bishop which was sent to Beijing on 22 September and in which the Most Illustrious Lord feigned indifference, despite what had been written to him about the disturbances happening in the missions and the danger to the Shandong mission, which was close by. He wrote in reply only the following: that the Fathers say they have accepted the royal permit without any condition etc. “I cannot comment on it,” he said, “since I was not present, but it can be understood that it was accepted by them without any condition, and so nothing prevents them from proceeding with caution and I have so advised them.” So says the Most Illustrious Bishop of Beijing.

It should be known, while we are on the subject, particularly with regard to the purgatorial oath of Fr. Jartoux, that there were other charges to which he replied. Indeed, as I advised above, the originals of that were sent to the French procuratorship, and then brought by the Fr. Provana to Rome.

Already here and there, not only in the province of Shandong but also and especially in Guangdong, many denied acts which nobody had considered deniable because they were no less certain and public than it was certain and public that those who had done them were living. Since those denying them were men of no small standing, their denial affected the minds of others and began to overturn the proofs and documents of others, not without great confusion. Hence others wrote to Beijing so that doubts aroused should be solved by certain knowledge. Others, so that they could do something about it, both for the sake of their consciences and to save the mission, asked for testimonies. To satisfy both, Fr. Antoine Thomas, the Vice-Rector of Beijing and substitute Vice- Provincial ordered in the following document that Fr. Kilian Stumpf, Procurator of the Vice-Province and Notary Apostolic, should by means of authentic documents correctly drawn up satisfy enquirers, as often as it was legitimately requested and in the form to be laid out below.

### 19. Concerning the catechism of Father Mezzafalce

Regarding the catechism of the Reverend Father Mezzafalce, I do not know whether it is expedient to say much, for it neither pertains to Beijing nor is its little history finished this month, since it was made and printed in the preceding year, and copies of it were not found in Beijing until October 1708. However, since there is no doubt that this and similar works, like the two catechisms of the Most Illustrious Bishop Maigrot, are cited in Europe, I will begin to narrate it at greater length.[^p630-20] Although I may seem to the reader to discuss such matters in an inopportune place, everyone will know how to accommodate the narration to his own convenience. |

### 20. The old dispute on the right expression of the form of baptism in Chinese

I repeat a matter dealt with earlier. On account of the intrinsic ambiguity of the Chinese language many missionaries in the past worried over how the form of baptism could be more correctly expressed.[^p630-21] This is shown by their tracts, with which they filled both China and the Archive of the Sacred

<!-- PDF page 631 | book page 621 -->

Congregation. Finally, they used one which was not disapproved by the Roman authorities. At the beginning of this century the Rev. Fr. Basilio da Glemona of the Franciscan Order, Vicar Apostolic in the province of Shaanxi, wanted to repudiate it, attacking others in written tracts which he communicated to the missionaries of all the Orders, proposing seven alternative formulas of baptism.[^p631-22] Most supported sticking to the old formula and with long and serious reasons represented it to the Reverend Father. The Most Illustrious Bishop of Ascalon, Fr. Joachim Bouvet of the Society of Jesus, and the Rev. Fr. Muñoz, a Dominican,[^p631-23] declared that the form used hitherto was valid and refuted the seven new ones he had thought up.

### 21. Father Mezzafalce composed a new formula

Reverend Fr. Basilio died from his labours and Rev. Fr. Mezzafalce, scarcely six years in China, produced his own new formula. This goes: Wo xi ni yi Fu, qie Zi, qie Sheng Shen, san wei yige Tianzhu ming or san wei yiti ming.[^p631-24] That is: I baptise you in, or for, or on account of the Father, and the Son, and the Holy Spirit, in the name of, or the name of, the three persons of the Lord of Heaven; or in the name of three persons of one substance. To the formula he added an explanation, wishing to prove that the chief difficulty in this matter consisted in the <!-- PDF page 632 | book page 622 | manuscript page 712 --> understanding or the expression of the meaning of the words in the name. And so by citing some texts of the Fathers, Councils, and St. Augustine, he concluded that those words were placed in the Latin formula because the minister of this Sacrament acts in the person of Christ, not the person of the Most Holy Trinity.

### 22. This was attacked by Fr. Muñoz the Dominican

But the Rev. Fr. Muñoz in his treatise mentioned above, examining the seven alternatives of the Rev. Fr. Basilio, showed that even the more recent formula of the Most Rev. Fr. Mezzafalce did not remove the difficulty, which more precisely lay in whether the Chinese word ming was singular or plural, signifying either the name or the names, and not whether the minister acts in the person of Christ or of the Most Holy Trinity. Even though the Divine Augustine says that Christ baptizes, this in no way excludes as joint causes the other members of the Most Holy Trinity, the minister, etc. It could be added that if the difficulty was what the Most Reverend Father brought forward, this seemed to increase in the new formula in which it was doubly asserted that the baptism was in the name of the Most Holy Trinity, and less clearly stated that the minister acted in the Person of Christ.

Going further, Fr. Muñoz rejected the formula mentioned because: 1. Fr. Mezzafalce replaced the Chinese yin with yi, and yi in Chinese does not seem to have the same force as the preposition yin (in)[^p632-25] does for Latin speakers. It can be translated for or on account of. It is used in the Chinese language when something is done for the benefit of another etc. But in, in the Latin formula, denotes virtue, power, authority, etc.

2. Because he says that the words san wei, “three persons,” are superfluous since each person is previously named separately, and therefore there is no need to refer to them together which will only cause confusion. This is neither a universal nor a particular usage in the Church up to the present.

3. Because it does not make clear whether the Chinese word means name, singular, or names, plural, since the numeral yige, “one,” does not qualify the word ming, “name,” | but Tianzhu, “Lord of Heaven,” which is singular, and the same goes for the alternative ending yi ti, “one substance.” Therefore, there remains the same doubt about the plural or singular meaning of the Chinese word ming, “name(s),” which is never understood to be singular unless some further indication is given, which in practice is what happens in China and has always been done up to the present when explaining what God and the Church <!-- PDF page 633 | book page 623 --> want to express by this term. But to us Europeans, brought up from childhood in the Latin language and the doctrine of the catechism, it more naturally occurs in the formula of baptism to say in the name to signify the one God indistinguishable in substance and three in person which are expressed there. But if any Chinese person starts to learn the Latin language and the rudiments of the catechism, and is not otherwise instructed in the correct Catholic meaning of those words in the name, they could easily fall into some doubt, just as Europeans exercising themselves in Chinese grammar do regarding the meaning of the Chinese word ming. What daily use does for Europeans, the same daily exercise makes clearer to the Chinese day by day, and they are more easily and fully instructed by keeping and explaining the ancient formula rather than by deforming through novelties the notions they have acquired up to the present.

### 23. Fr. Serravalle of his own accord gives up the office of Pro-Vicar

Now some matters closely related to the catechism. As previously noted Rev. Fr. Mezzafalce was still living quietly at home and had communicated briefly with us when the Most Illustrious and Reverend Lord Patriarch of Antioch arrived in his capacity as Apostolic Visitor. In the month of April 1705, he came with Rev. Fr. Serravalle of the Order of St. Francis, whom the Most Illustrious Lord Bishop of Beirut[^p633-26] had made when he was about to leave for China his Pro-Vicar in the province of Huguang. Fr. Serravalle in a memorable example renounced this office into the hands of the Lord Patriarch, saying that he preferred to learn first what should be done rather than, in ignorance, to order others what was to be done. The Most Illustrious Lord Patriarch, having accepted the resignation, immediately—within a month—designated Fr. Mezzafalce as the new Pro-Vicar.

### 24. Fr. Mezzafalce created Vicar Apostolic

It happened that Rev. Fr. Alcalá of the Order of St. Dominic died completely unexpectedly. He was Apostolic Vicar in Zhejiang and the Lord Patriarch ordered that Fr. Mezzafalce, leaving the pro-vicariate of the province of Huguang, should succeed the Reverend Father de Alcalá, piously dead in the Lord. All these things were hastily done in <!-- PDF page 634 | book page 624 | manuscript page 713 --> the months of August and September 1705. Immediately after the arrival of the Lord Patriarch, and among these new dignities, the Rev. Fr. Mezzafalce exercised himself industriously in composing various pages, printing them, and publishing them. We shall perhaps say a few things about this after the catechism.

### 25. A new and erroneous baptismal formula is invented by Fr. Mezzafalce

In the following year, 1706, the Most Rev. Father himself rejected the above mentioned Formula and inserted a new one in the catechism he had compiled. This catechism has 15 folios, 40 single pages, and 128 characters to a full page. When finished it was printed, in a few copies only, some time before October 1706.

The new Formula of Baptism is the following:

Wo xi yu yin Shenfu qie Shengzi qie Shengshen mingzhe,[^p634-27] “I baptize you in the name of the Holy Father, the Holy Son, and the Holy Spirit.” To these words is added a question of one desiring to be taught: Shenme yisi,[^p634-28] “What do these words mean to you?” And the learner replies: | Ye shi Yesu suo dingde Jingyan,[^p634-29] “These also are the words laid down by Jesus in the Gospels (or Scriptures).”

### 26. Delated to Rome by the Fathers

Since these are undeniably contained in the said catechism and are indisputably erroneous in theology, the Fathers sent the catechism itself to Rome, not for it to be censured by the tribunal but so that its author would take care to instruct himself and seriously try to repair, insofar as he could, the great harm that an error of this kind had caused or could cause in the simple minds of ordinary Christians.

For doing this we have been accused of two things: 1. That we did not refer the matter to His Lordship the Patriarch who was then living in Macau. 2. That we seem to have presumed that the Reverend Father was really the author of the aforementioned catechism, but there was a presumption in his favour on the grounds of his reputation for theological knowledge and his acquaintance with the language and Chinese characters. There was also his office as Vicar Apostolic, which he had received as the most worthy and had so far exercised zealously.

<!-- PDF page 635 | book page 625 -->

### 27. Reasons why it was sent to Rome

Re 1. The author had already left China, and was now subject neither to the tribunal nor the correction of the Lord Patriarch. Nor did the Fathers think they could gain more in this matter for the common good from the Lord Patriarch than they received on other occasions when they warned His Excellency about other writings of Fr. Mezzafalce which involved the abuse of Chinese characters in print. They did so without any accusation and the only result was that they were considered calumniators, even though the printed errors could not be denied.

Re 2. His reputation for European learning and Chinese erudition, whether true or false, did not prevent the Most Rev. Fr. Mezzafalce being modestly and deservedly refuted by Rev. Fr. Muñoz of the Order of St. Dominic, as I have mentioned above. But if during the two years immediately preceding the author, whether in a European language or Chinese, had written about the same or similar matters something reprehensible, it can be presumed that he has erred again, for there is no evidence that he has acquired a more sublime knowledge of theology or of the Chinese language, and although he has obtained the office of Vicar Apostolic this neither confers learning nor increases it.

Also that catechism ought to have been sent to Rome, because after the refutation of the former the Most Reverend Father did not correct the error but changed it into another and now published it with authority. For he had prepared the catechism so that during his visitation through his province in the year 1707 he might distribute it to the Christians as a model to be followed in virtue of his appointment, and it was already being placed in the hands of the catechumens. In proof of which …30

### 28. Why Fr. Mezzafalce is rightly believed to be the author of the catechism and the formula

Too powerful were the many documents which go to prove that the Most Reverend Father brought out the said book. For 1. The printing blocks were found in his house. 2. Printed copies came out from his house alone. 3. His servants whenever asked by both missionaries and Christians asserted openly and without hesitation that the blocks were carved by order of their master the Most Reverend Father Mezzafalce. 4. The same was testified by the prefect of the Church and Christian affairs, in whose care the tablets remained deposited. 5. The cutter of the blocks, who is still living, gave the same testimony. In the Appendix each of the testimonies is to be found at length.

<!-- PDF page 636 | book page 626 | manuscript page 714 -->

Nevertheless, all these do not prove that the said catechism was composed by the Most Reverend Father himself, although they do persuade us that he promoted their printing. Indeed, it is clear that not everything was composed by him, for the largest part is found in other earlier publications. | But that is not what is at issue. It is enough that the new formula of baptism was produced under his care, at his order, at his expense, and by authority of his own office.

### 29. The pagans attack the churches destitute of missionaries

Many letters have been written this month to Beijing about what both mandarins and people dared to do against churches and missionaries. Among others the Rev. Fr. Francisco Cantero,[^p636-31] Provincial Vicar of the Dominican Fathers wrote to Fr. Antoine Thomas on the 17th of August that in the province of Fujian churches deprived of missionaries were being confiscated. Because of this desolation in a holy place, he asked for help to be brought. I add here a copy of this letter.

### 30. Letter of Fr. Francisco Cantero, Dominican, on disasters to religion

Reverend Father, Dear Sir, Pax Christi, I wrote Your Reverence a letter from the city of Hangzhou and I hope it arrived safely. May I next wish both of us good health insofar as the common ailments of old age allow and pay my respects to Your Reverence.

I find myself in this city with five other Fathers of our mission to carry out the royal orders for us to depart from China for reasons Your Reverence will already know. The mandarins are giving us much trouble, the leader being the fuyuan of Guangzhou from whom we received recently an order that in accordance with the royal decree we should leave here within five days. But since a suitable boat was not available and the time laid down for our departure was running out, the magistrate of this city replied that for now he cannot carry out the said order but that when the time comes he will certainly carry it out, and we will depart for wherever Divine Providence should direct us.

We have just received news from the Christians of the province of Fujian that the mandarins have closed and sealed the churches, which brings me to the role that your Reverend Fathers play in the metropolis of Fuzhou, to request that they assist those poor Christians who are deeply troubled at seeing themselves deprived of their ministers. Given that there is no royal order that the churches should be treated this way, it seemed to me appropriate to write to

<!-- PDF page 637 | book page 627 | manuscript page 715, 716 -->

Your Reverence to intervene with the authorities and to see what remedy there can be to repair such damage, and to provide as in the past such consolation as is possible to give to these sad and afflicted Christians. All of this is the work of Satan who tries to achieve by his poisonous stratagems the destruction of souls. But I hope that through the charity and zeal of Your Reverence something can be done about such a great disaster, with the aid of whatever Providence suggests in the Lord and by enlisting your mandarin friends in this court to write to those of Fujian not to go beyond what His Majesty has ordered. Would you, for the love of God, advise me as early as possible that you have taken on this task so that from here I can advise the said Christians of the favour Your Reverence has done for them. I commend you and all the Fathers of the Company in my prayers and await your instructions, in the hope that you will let me know how I can be of service.

In the meantime may God preserve me and yours.

Macau, 17 August 1707.

Your Reverence’s least and most obedient servant,

J.S.M.B. Fray Francisco Cantero.
